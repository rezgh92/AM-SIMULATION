    - **question**: Is the binder decomposition single-step or multi-step, and does E(alpha) vary?
    - **answer**: Multi-step, and structurally worse than the recon's DAEM framing assumes. There are at least three physically distinct processes with different governing physics, not just a spread of activation energies within one mechanism: (1) solvent evaporation (vapour-liquid equilibrium + capillary/Darcy transport, near-zero apparent E during a constant-rate period, so isoconversional analysis will return a spurious E(alpha) at low alpha); (2) acrylate/methacrylate network depolymerisation; (3) char gasification. Roumanie's TGA of four acrylate resins shows ~7 wt% char residue remaining at 600 C in argon versus complete burnout in air - i.e. the NUMBER of steps, not merely the rates, changes with atmosphere, which a single p(E) cannot represent. Independently, Incledon's thesis documents ceramic-binder TGA in which mass loss shifts to HIGHER temperature as heating rate increases (anti-Arrhenius), and states this 'makes it nearly impossible to develop a model with a single set of kinetics parameters that fit all of the data if an Arrhenius activated process is being modeled'. Use the ICTAC 2020 multi-step recommendations, which the recon did not cite, not only the 2011 general one.

    - **question**: Does the binder decomposition mechanism change between inert (Ar), reducing (Ar/H2) and slightly oxidising atmospheres?

    - **question**: Is binder removal for this geometry diffusion-controlled or permeation(pressure)-controlled?
    - **answer**: Neither, for realistic LMM copper - the pressure constraint is essentially never active, which invalidates the load-bearing constraint of the recon's optimal-control formulation. Conclusion: the pressure/cracking constraint binds only in the narrow early window where the molten binder still saturates the pore space; after ~5% conversion the ramp can be far more aggressive than the ceramic literature would suggest. - **actionable_for_architecture**:
  - INVERT THE CONTROL HIERARCHY: make oxidant activity, not temperature rate, the primary control variable. Roumanie's screen shows a ~20x swing in residual carbon from atmosphere alone while temperature (400/600/800 C), dwell (4/7/10 h) and pH2 (50/400/600 mbar) changed nothing. Practically that means a mass-flow-controller / gas-mixing rig with a zirconia oxygen sensor and a dew-point meter in the exhaust, which is cheaper and far easier to retrofit than slaving furnace temperature to a shrinkage signal. If only one closed loop gets built, build this one, not CRTA.
  - ADD TWO MISSING STATES BEFORE ADDING ANY DISCREPANCY MODEL. The state vector must carry (i) a solvent-evaporation variable with evaporation/capillary physics (NOT an Arrhenius alpha), and (ii) a copper oxidation extent chi_ox with its own parabolic kinetics (Q = 111 kJ/mol above 873 K, 40 kJ/mol below 773 K) and a reduction sink that releases H2O. The binding constraints for copper LMM are: (a) residual C at the moment of pore closure; (b) oxide-reduction completeness before pore closure (hydrogen embrittlement / steam blistering once porosity is closed - Sandia's copper hydrogen-compatibility reference makes the mechanism explicit); (c) hot green strength versus self-weight and friction (Sandia's brown copper bars FRACTURED rather than bent); (d) distortion. Use that measured profile as the warm start / initial guess for the direct-transcription solve; it is a free, industrially validated feasible point for a copper system with an undisclosed binder.
  - BREAK THE sigma_s0 / eta0 DEGENERACY WITH A LOADED EXPERIMENT - AND FOR COPPER, GRAVITY IS THE LOAD. Free-sintering dilatometry identifies only the ratio sigma_s0/eta0 (exact structural degeneracy, FIM condition number 1.5e12, verified here). Classical sinter-forging is one fix. But for copper the cheaper and more informative fix is a gravity-loaded unsupported-overhang bend test, because the body force is independent of sigma_s0 while shrinkage depends on sigma_s0/eta0 - so shrinkage plus deflection together identify both. This is exactly what Sandia did (bending bars on support blocks, vertical displacement used to calibrate viscoplasticity, density used for grain growth). Budget for the fact that brown copper bars will fracture: pre-sinter on a flat setter first, and note pre-sintering cost Sandia ~6 points of ultimate density.
  - DOWNGRADE THE SENSITIVITY/SURROGATE CAMPAIGN AND UPGRADE THE SEMI-EMPIRICAL INVERSION. Do not run Morris -> Sobol' -> sparse PCE -> GP on a 12-parameter SOVS. Instead: (1) fix Olevsky's seven canonical closure constants; (2) recover eta0(T) POINTWISE from dilatometry by Balaguer's inversion, using the physical criterion that eta0 must be single-valued in T on heating and cooling; (3) fit the two grain-growth parameters (b5, rho_c) by BFGS with a5 = 1, priors b5 ~ 1.19-1.28, rho_c ~ 0.93-0.94; (4) only then run profile likelihood on the 2-4 surviving parameters. Keep the Morris/Sobol' machinery for the debinding+oxidation block, where the parameter count really is large and the physics really is uncertain.
  - USE PARK'S GRAIN-GROWTH-GENERALISED MSC, NOT SU-JOHNSON MSC, AND ONLY AS A DIAGNOSTIC. Kerbart/Maniere state explicitly that the conventional unique-trajectory assumption 'prevents from any sintering optimization', and show Q varying 400-700 kJ/mol across the density range of one material. The cheap level of the multi-fidelity hierarchy should be the closed-form free-sintering ODE xi_dot = -3*sigma_s0*xi/(4*eta0) from SAND2017 Eq. 31 (analytic, differentiable, CasADi-ready), not an MSC with a single fitted Q.
  - SET THE TEMPORAL DISCRETISATION FLOOR AND THE INTEGRATOR CHOICE FROM THE VERIFICATION REPORT. SAND2017 shows dt = 600 s (5 steps over a 250 C / 3000 s ramp) gives visible error while dt = 15 s (200 steps) reproduces the analytic solution, and recommends backward Euler (beta = 1.0) ONLY - beta = 0.5 resolves deformation but produces 'unstable, oscillatory responses in stress that are not only undesirable but also non-physical'. Use beta = 1.0 and dt <= 15 s inside any optimiser loop, and reproduce the free-sinter / sinter-forge verification case before touching copper. Note you will need theta0 from Arguello 2009 (doi:10.1111/j.1551-2916.2009.03008.x) to do so - Table 1 of the Sandia report omits it.
  - MODEL SHRINKAGE ANISOTROPY EXPLICITLY, NOT AS ISOTROPIC SCALING PLUS DISTORTION. Measured copper BPE shrinkage is 16% horizontal versus 19% vertical. Treating the 3-point difference as 'distortion' to be compensated by a GP is wasteful; carry an anisotropic sintering strain tensor keyed to the build direction (which in LCM is a real, physical layer-wise density/cure gradient) and let the GP handle only the residual, geometry-dependent warpage.
  - PUT FRICTION IN THE MODEL AT THE RIGHT MAGNITUDE. Measured static friction between brown sinter-based AM parts and alumina setter plates at 1100 C is 0.87-1.31 (std dev up to 0.14), not the 0.1-0.3 usually assumed, and static rises much more than dynamic with temperature. Setter-plate condition (number of prior cycles) mattered more than roughness or density. Treat mu as an uncertain parameter with a wide prior centred near 1.0, carry setter-plate service history as a covariate, and include it in the Sobol' screen for distortion - it will very likely rank first alongside green-density gradient.
  - BUILD THE PAPER AROUND THE ATMOSPHERE-TRAJECTORY OPTIMAL CONTROL PROBLEM, NOT AROUND A DAEM FIT. There is essentially no published Bayesian calibration / UQ / Bayesian-OED work on debinding or sintering of AM parts (Crossref searches in this session returned nothing on point), so the methodological novelty is available. But the strongest and most defensible contribution is the two-oxidant-window result: a computed, thermodynamically-grounded, experimentally-anchored map of the (T, pO2, pH2O/pH2) region that gasifies binder char while keeping copper reducible, combined with a pore-closure deadline for oxide reduction, solved as a multi-phase optimal control problem. That is a genuinely new cycle-design principle for copper, whereas 'we fitted a DAEM to a proprietary binder' is not.
  - SEQUENCE THE EXPERIMENTS BY INFORMATION, NOT BY THE BOED MACHINERY. Order: (1) TGA to constant mass in air AND in Ar on printed green coupons - gives total organics, char fraction and the atmosphere effect in one run; (2) a 4-cell atmosphere screen (air-400C; Ar; Ar-4%H2; staged air-then-H2) on printed coupons with C/O combustion analysis - this is the axis carrying nearly all the information; (3) TGA at >=5 heating rates in the TWO atmospheres that survive, with isoconversional pre-analysis per ICTAC 2020; (4) dilatometry for eta0(T) inversion; (5) gravity-loaded bend bars to break the sigma_s0/eta0 degeneracy; (6) a two-thickness-calibrate / third-thickness-predict transfer test for the discrepancy model. Only after step 3 is there a design space worth running nested-Monte-Carlo EIG on. Do NOT buy gas permeametry - the permeability is bounded at 1e-15 to 1e-13 m2 and the pressure constraint is inactive in that range.
  - MODEL PART-TO-PART VARIABILITY HIERARCHICALLY AT THE PRINT-BATCH LEVEL. Roumanie's four formulations with near-identical TGA gave sintered densities of 92+/-3, 82+/-3, 92+/-1 and 94+/-1 %, with the spread attributed to polymerisation stress and cracking rather than kinetics, and the F3/F4 difference caused solely by the photoinitiator. Scheibler's density series is single-specimen. Sandia lost 6 density points to an unexplained pre-sintering effect. Put a random effect on print batch and a second on furnace run; do not fold this into an i.i.d. sigma.
  - TRACK BINDER-DERIVED METALLIC CONTAMINANTS AS A FIRST-CLASS OUTPUT, NOT AN AFTERTHOUGHT. Roumanie's BAPO photoinitiator put 0.035 wt% P into the final copper and cut thermal conductivity from ~250-270 to ~120 W/m.K, invisible in TGA. Lithoz will not disclose their initiator package. Specify ICP-OES on sintered coupons (P, Ti, Co, Fe, As, Si, S) as a routine QoI alongside C and O, and make the functional property (thermal/electrical conductivity), not density, the terminal objective in the OCP - density can be 95% and the part still useless if P is present.
  - MAKE CONTACT WITH THE PEOPLE WHO ALREADY HAVE THIS DATA. Incus GmbH (the Lithoz metal spin-out, Vienna) with TU Wien presented 'Recent Advances Of Lithography-based Metal Manufacturing Of Copper' (Anand Atul, C. Gierl-Mayer, G. Harakaly) at Euro PM2024, Malmoe, on exactly copper feedstock/processability/sintered properties by LMM. Fraunhofer IFAM Dresden (Scheibler, Studnitzky, Zhong, Weissgaerber) and CEA-LITEN Grenoble (Roumanie, Laucournet) have published the two closest datasets. A single conversation with any of them is worth more than several months of BOED.
