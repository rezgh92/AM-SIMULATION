- **actionable_for_architecture**:
  - SPLIT THE PROBLEM AT THE CLOSED-POROSITY THRESHOLD. The entire physics changes at roughly 92-95 % relative density: before it the body is open-porous, gas-permeable, thermally insulating (k_eff about 1-10 W/m/K) and safe in hydrogen; after it, gas transport stops, conductivity jumps toward 400 W/m/K, and any residual internal oxide becomes a steam-void / hydrogen-embrittlement hazard. Build the solver as two coupled regimes with an explicit pore-closure event, and make 'internal oxygen below threshold BEFORE pore closure' a hard constraint in the optimiser, not a post-hoc check.
  - MAKE DEW POINT A FIRST-CLASS CONTROL VARIABLE ALONGSIDE T(t) AND GAS SPECIES. The thermodynamics I computed show the feasible window is enormous and asymmetric: at 1000 C the Cu/Cu2O boundary tolerates pH2O/pH2 up to 1.1e4, while decarburization (C + H2O = CO + H2) only becomes favourable above roughly 700-750 C (K = 125 at 1050 C). The optimal cycle therefore has two atmosphere windows - oxidising at 350-450 C to burn the bulk of the carbon, then WET reducing at 850-1050 C to gasify the residue and strip oxygen - with a dry-out before final densification and cool-down. This is exactly what TU Wien/Incus found experimentally (Euro PM2025, DOI 10.59499/ep256778885). The control vector is u(t) = [T, dT/dt, pO2 or pH2, pH2O (dew point), total pressure], and the furnace must have a logged dew-point sensor.
  - SOLVE CONJUGATE HEAT TRANSFER WITH AN EXOTHERMIC, OXYGEN-TRANSPORT-LIMITED SOURCE TERM. Do not assume the green body is isothermal. k_eff is about 1 W/m/K in the green state and evolves by orders of magnitude through debinding and sintering, and the organics carry about 2.5 MJ per kg of green body. The debinding model needs (i) a percolation/effective-medium k_eff(phi, conversion, density), (ii) an O2 diffusion equation from the surface inward, (iii) a DSC-calibrated heat of reaction, (iv) a Damkohler-number criterion flagging thermal runaway. Hot spots, not gas pressure alone, are a credible crack mechanism in an air-debound copper part.
  - MODEL THE SOLVENT AS EVAPORATION PLUS PLASTICISATION, NOT AS A DAEM PSEUDO-COMPONENT. The LCM solvent is a high-boiling phthalate-class ester at about 44 wt% of the organic phase whose removal is vapour-pressure- and diffusion-controlled and which shifts the polymer pyrolysis window by up to 135 C. The correct formulation is a coupled two-phase transport problem (Clausius-Clapeyron plus Fickian diffusion in the swollen network plus Darcy flow in the opening pore network), with the DAEM applied only to the crosslinked fraction and its parameters made a function of residual solvent content.
  - BUILD THE STATE VECTOR AROUND THE FOUR VARIABLES THAT ACTUALLY DETERMINE COPPER PROPERTIES: carbon, oxygen, phosphorus (plus germanium if Ivocerin), and relative density. Everything else is instrumental. Target values from the Incus benchmark: 95.5 % density, 93.8 % IACS, 385 W/m/K. From Roumanie's data the achievable carbon is 0.013-0.022 wt% (powder level) and the stubborn variable is oxygen at 0.062-0.085 wt% versus 0.028 in the powder. The objective should be a weighted combination of density, conductivity and crack probability, with C, O and P as constrained state variables.
  - COUPLE THE PRINT PARAMETERS TO THE DEBINDING MODEL - THEY ARE NOT SEPARABLE. Maniere et al. show experimentally that printed LAYER THICKNESS is the second-strongest predictor of debinding cracking after wall thickness (20 um layers debound 9-11 mm cubes that 30 and 40 um layers cracked), because interlayer conversion gradients create planes of weakness. With copper's very shallow cure depth at 450 nm this effect will be amplified. The simulation input must therefore carry layer thickness, exposure dose and the resulting through-layer degree-of-cure profile as an anisotropic, layered initial condition - an orthotropic green body with reduced interlayer cohesive strength - not a homogeneous continuum.
  - ADOPT A TWO-LAYER MODEL STACK: PHYSICS FOR THE CONTINUUM FIELDS, A SMALL-DOE ML CLASSIFIER FOR THE CRACK YES/NO DECISION. The Open Ceramics paper achieves 0.911 accuracy from 45 grid-designed experiments using only three geometric features, and argues explicitly that calorimetry-driven DAEM plus thermomechanical FE cannot capture VPP layer architecture. Mirror that: use FE for temperature, conversion, gas pressure, stress and shrinkage fields, then train a classifier on physics-derived features (max pore pressure, max tensile stress, max dT/dx, integrated O2 flux) plus geometric features against a deliberately designed cracking DOE. This is also the most publishable architecture - nobody has done it for a metal.
  - SEQUENCE THE EXPERIMENTS SO THE MODEL IS CALIBRATED BEFORE IT IS TRUSTED. Weeks 1-2: slurry density plus reduce-and-weigh burnout (Ar to 600 C then Ar-5%H2 to constant mass) for w_s, rho_b, phi_s; He pycnometry and laser diffraction on recovered powder; ASTM E159 hydrogen loss on virgin powder; ICP-OES for P and Ge on virgin powder AND green body. Weeks 3-4: multi-rate TGA-MS/FTIR at 1/2/5/10/20 K/min in Ar, air and Ar-5%H2 on FILLED green bodies of at least 50 mg; DSC for heat of reaction; headspace GC-MS for solvent identity. Weeks 5-6: green density and permeability (micro-CT plus gas permeametry); dilatometry of the brown body UNDER LOAD for sintering viscosity and sinter strain - the Tischel & Ploshikhin route (Euro PM2024, DOI 10.59499/ep246278578) determines these directly from loaded dilatometry and cuts the experimental burden drastically. Only then fit the DAEM and the SOVS/MSC parameters.
  - BUILD THE INPUT FILE WITH EXPLICIT UNCERTAINTY AND PROPAGATE IT. The error analysis is unambiguous: d(phi)/d(w_s) = 2.567 versus d(phi)/d(rho_b) = 0.227, so a 0.5 wt% error in the burnout mass fraction costs 1.3 vol% in solids loading while a 0.02 g/cm3 error in binder density costs only 0.45 vol%. Spend the metrology budget on the mass balance. Then run the optimiser as ROBUST optimisation (worst-case or CVaR over the posterior of phi, rho_b, char yield and permeability) rather than point optimisation - with a proprietary binder you will never have a point estimate, and a cycle optimal only at nominal parameters is worthless.
  - RESOLVE THE LTCC-CO-FIRE VERSUS STANDALONE-COPPER BRANCH BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING. Lithoz's own 2025 material folder presents the copper capability of the 2M30 in an LTCC context (copper and silver co-printed with glass ceramics, conductor tracks embedded in dielectric ceramic in a single production step). If the slurry is formulated for co-firing at 850-1000 C in nitrogen, the powder is finer, the binder burns out below the glass softening point, and the problem becomes CONSTRAINED sintering with co-firing mismatch stresses - a different and arguably more publishable simulation problem than free sintering. Architect the solver so the constitutive layer is pluggable: free sintering (SOVS with sinter stress and bulk/shear viscosity) versus constrained co-sintering (two-phase domain with an interface and a mismatch-strain history).
  - TREAT THE OPTIMISER AS A CONSTRAINED OPTIMAL-CONTROL PROBLEM, NOT A PARAMETER SWEEP. Minimise total cycle time (or maximise final conductivity) subject to: max tensile stress below green strength everywhere and at all times; max pore pressure below a fracture criterion; internal oxygen below threshold before pore closure; pO2 on the metallic side of the Cu/Cu2O line at all times above 200 C; furnace ramp-rate and dew-point hardware limits. The control is a piecewise-linear T(t) with segment-wise atmosphere - which is exactly what a furnace program is - so the optimiser's output is directly executable. Adjoint gradients over the FE model, or a Gaussian-process surrogate if the FE is too slow; the surrogate route also supplies the uncertainty needed for the robust formulation.
  - INSTRUMENT FOR VALIDATION FROM THE START, BECAUSE THE PAPER LIVES OR DIES ON IT. Minimum viable validation set: (a) a thermogravimetric run on a FULL-SIZE part on a furnace load cell, not just a 50 mg TGA coupon - small-sample TGA cannot reproduce the gas-transport limitation and this is the commonest failure in the literature; (b) in-situ dilatometry through the whole cycle; (c) micro-CT of green, brown and sintered states on the same specimen for shrinkage-field validation; (d) LECO C and O plus ICP-OES P at green, brown and sintered states to close the elemental mass balance; (e) a deliberately cracked specimen set spanning wall thickness 1-10 mm and layer thickness 20/30/40 um to train and test the crack classifier. A closed elemental mass balance plus a validated shrinkage field plus a crack classifier, all on an UNKNOWN proprietary binder, is a genuinely novel contribution.
  - EXPLOIT THE 2M30's MULTI-MATERIAL CAPABILITY AS A CHEAP EXPERIMENTAL MULTIPLIER, NOT JUST AS A PRODUCT FEATURE. The machine can assign individual pixels within one layer to different slurries (Nohut's method M3) and can dose slurry into the vat between layers (method M1). That means one build can carry a full geometric DOE - wall thicknesses 1-10 mm, layer thicknesses 20/30/40 um, and graded Cu/ceramic interfaces - which is exactly the grid-structured, small-N dataset the ML crack classifier needs. Design the very first copper build as a DOE coupon plate, not as a demonstrator part.
