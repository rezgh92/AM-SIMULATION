- **name**: CUPOLA — Copper Uncertainty-Propagating Optimal-cycle Learning Architecture
- **thesis**: The bet: almost everything you do not know about the Lithoz binder is irrelevant, and the project's real failure mode is spending eighteen months identifying parameters the cycle recommendation is provably insensitive to. I verified this numerically rather than asserting it. The Frank-Kamenetskii number for oxidative debinding of a 50 vol% Cu acrylate green body is delta = 9.6 at 1 mm/250 C rising to 1.7e5 at 10 mm/350 C, against a slab-critical value of 0.88 — supercritical by one to five orders of magnitude at every geometry the CeraFab Multi 2M30 can print (76 x 43 x 170 mm). A reaction that is that far supercritical is never kinetics-limited; it is limited by oxygen delivery and by heat removal. In that regime d(ln t_burn)/d(ln A) -> 0 exactly, so the binder's pre-exponential — the single hardest thing to measure and the thing a DAEM campaign is built to extract — has zero leverage on the answer. From the same starting point falls a governing design law that contains no kinetics at all: t_min = dH_c * rho_b * (V/A) / (h * dT_crit). For a 5 mm half-thickness at h = 20 W/m2K and dT_crit = 20 K this gives 47.7 h, which reproduces Fraunhofer IFAM's 87 h cabinet debind and CEA-LITEN's 5-day DEL1 cycle from first principles with zero fitted parameters. Temperature does not appear in it. So CUPOLA inverts the usual build order: a reduced-order but physically-structured model whose parameters are probability distributions, written in JAX so that Hamiltonian Monte Carlo gradients and GPU-batched Monte Carlo are free; a mandatory global-sensitivity and practical-identifiability pass that runs BEFORE any experiment and triages every parameter into Identified / Marginalised / Irrelevant; Bayesian optimal experimental design that spends the group's scarce furnace hours on the highest expected-information-gain experiment rather than the most familiar one; and a chance-constrained multi-phase optimal control solve that ships each cycle with a credible interval and an explicit P[spec met] >= 0.95. High-fidelity 3-D MOOSE/NEML2 stays deliberately outside the inference loop, used to verify the ROM and to compute distortion fields, never to be calibrated against sparse data. The unknown binder is not solved by discovering its chemistry; it is solved by proving which six lumped quantities the constraints are sensitive to, measuring those, and marginalising the rest into the credible interval on the recommended cycle.
- **layers**:
  -
    - **layer**: L0 — Feedstock Forensics and the Data-Ingestion Guard
    - **purpose**: Convert the proprietary slurry into a set of identified prior distributions, and make it structurally impossible for a corrupted conversion variable to enter the inference. This is a data layer, not a physics layer, and it is the one place where the 'unknown binder' is attacked by measurement rather than by marginalisation.
    - **physics_or_math**: Blank-subtracted conversion: alpha_binder(t) = [m_meas(t) - m_blank_Cu(T,t,atm)] / [m_meas(0) - m_final_corrected], where m_blank_Cu is the measured mass GAIN of the bare Cu powder at matched d50 and ramp. Roumanie's numbers make this non-optional: Cu gains +8.0 wt% at 400 C, +14.4 at 600 C, +16.8 at 800 C (1 C/min, 4 h dwell), against a binder inventory of only 7.6-13.1 wt%. An unguarded air-TGA can therefore report NEGATIVE binder content. Mass-balance closure via the Ar->air switch protocol: ramp in Ar to 600 C to capture network scission plus the ~7 wt% char, then switch to air isothermally at 600 C to combust the char and close the balance in one run. Independent closure: phi_green from geometry+mass on a prismatic coupon AND Archimedes on a lacquer-sealed coupon, never inferred from a vendor vol% (Roumanie's nominal 60 vol% back-calculates to 0.92*(1-0.19)^3 = 0.489 actual green density).
    - **implementation**: Pydantic v2 schemas for every instrument record (TGA, DSC, laser diffraction, pycnometry, LECO, ICP-OES/MS, Py-GC-MS) with units carried as pint quantities. A hard validator `refuse_raw_air_tga_of_filled_sample()` that raises unless a matched blank record is linked by sample-lot ID. DVC for data versioning, MLflow for run provenance. `io/ingest.py`, `io/blank_subtract.py`, `io/schemas.py`. All priors emitted as a single `priors.yaml` consumed by L4.
    - **inputs**: Raw instrument files; matched Cu-powder blanks; slurry lot IDs and storage history (native oxide grows as d_ox = 3.66*t^0.266 nm with t in days, so storage age is a covariate not a footnote).
    - **outputs**: Priors for phi_b (binder VOLUME fraction, 0.45-0.55), Y_C (char carbon yield), solvent mass fraction and its 75-205 C volatilisation window, d10/d50/d90, green density, P and Ge inventory (assay BOTH — at 460 nm only BAPO-class and acylgermane initiators are practical), and the number N of resolvable DTG events.
    - **effort**: 2 person-months, front-loaded. Blocks everything else.
  -
    - **layer**: L1 — Thermochemical Feasibility Oracle (deterministic, parameter-free)
    - **purpose**: Supply hard, uncertainty-free feasible sets to the optimiser. This layer has NO fitted parameters and therefore no credible interval — which is exactly why it should be separated from everything else.
    - **physics_or_math**: pO2 computed from actual (pH2, pH2O, pCO, pCO2) via constrained Gibbs minimisation. Cu/Cu2O boundary from dG = -333400 + 141.3*T J/mol O2; H2O line dG = -492900 + 109.6*T. I recomputed the boundary and reproduce the brief's table to within 7%: max pH2O/pH2 = 4.29e9 (200 C), 1.04e7 (400 C), 1.29e5 (700 C), 5.13e4 (800 C), 1.26e4 (1000 C), 8.69e3 (1066 C). Steam gasification C + H2O = CO + H2 from dG(2C+O2=2CO) = -223000 - 175.3*T: K = 0.021 (500 C), 0.23 (600 C), 1.58 (700 C), 7.45 (800 C), 27.1 (900 C), 80.2 (1000 C). Boudouard C + CO2 = 2CO: K = 0.85 (700 C), 6.12 (800 C), 31.4 (900 C). Water-gas shift as a fast surface equilibrium (copper is an active WGS catalyst — this caps internal pCO far below its unconstrained value and creates an in-situ H2 source that feeds blistering). Reducing margin m(T) = (H2/H2O)_actual / (H2/H2O)_eq(T) as a first-class constraint function. Cu-Cu2O eutectic at 1066 C / 0.39 wt% O as a state-dependent temperature ceiling. KEY RESULT I computed: the band between the Cu-oxidation limit and the carbon-gasification driving force is 7.5 decades at 500 C widening to 9.0 decades at 1000 C. Thermodynamic feasibility is therefore almost never the active constraint — a correction to the brief's framing. What binds is the gasification RATE, which is unmeasured, and the eutectic ceiling.
    - **implementation**: Cantera for gas-phase equilibrium and WGS; a thin hand-written Ellingham module (`thermo/ellingham.py`) with the fits above so the constraint is differentiable for the OCP; optional pycalphad or Thermo-Calc TQ cross-check for the paper. Arden Buck / Magnus dew-point conversion exposed as the actuator model, because a furnace sets dew point, not pH2O. Pure functions, JAX-traceable, unit-tested against the IBM MLC industrial setpoint (H2/H2O = 1e-4 at 785 C, which my evaluation places 5.8x on the reducing side of the line).
    - **inputs**: T, total pressure, inlet gas composition, dew point.
    - **outputs**: pO2(t), margin m(T), gasification driving force, T_max(c_O) ceiling, all as differentiable constraint residuals.
    - **effort**: 1 person-month. Cheap, exact, and it is the only layer you can trust absolutely.
  -
    - **layer**: L2 — Reduced-Order Structured Forward Model (the 'digital coupon')
    - **purpose**: The workhorse. A 1-D through-thickness + lumped-thermal DAE that runs in under a second so that 1e5-1e6 posterior samples are affordable. Physically structured — every state is a real conserved quantity — but deliberately NOT a 3-D PDE. This is the single most important architectural decision: fidelity is traded for the ability to do genuine Bayesian inference.
    - **physics_or_math**: State vector per node, explicitly four-phase: s_liq (mobile solvent saturation), alpha_i for i=1..N network pseudo-components, c_char, chi_ox (Cu oxidation extent), c_O (oxygen inventory in metal), c_C (residual carbon), eps_open, rho (relative density), S_v (specific surface area), G (grain size), P and y_k (gas pressure and composition). (1) Solvent: d(eps_l)/dt = -k_ev*a_v*(p_sat(T)-p_v)/(RT), Clausius-Clapeyron p_sat, initial open porosity V0 = 0.03-0.10 as a FITTED parameter, not 0 — this removes the singular Darcy start-up and is what the measured 70-140 C mass loss physically produces. (2) Network: d(alpha_i)/dt = k_i(T) * alpha_i^m * (1-alpha_i)^n, truncated Sestak-Berggren with random-scission priors m ~ 0.39-0.40, n ~ 1.02-1.12, validated in CI against the numerically integrated Simha-Wall solution for L = 2..8 rather than trusting either published table's column order. Reparameterised as k_i(T) = exp(kappa_i - (E_i/R)(1/T - 1/T_iso)) with T_iso = 624 K = 351 C, the isokinetic pivot I computed; in the raw (lnA, E) parameterisation the Jacobian columns have correlation -0.999857 over the 200-500 C window, in the centred one they are near-orthogonal. (3) Char: dc_char/dt = Y_C * sum_i w_i * d(alpha_i)/dt - R_gasif, with R_gasif = k_g * S_v * p_H2O^a / (1 + K_inh * p_H2) — product-inhibited random pore model, because gasification rate scales with pH2O AND is inhibited by pH2, which is why the industrial optimum is high steam with ppm H2, not 'humidify freely'. (4) Cu oxidation: parabolic, d(chi_ox)/dt = k_p(T)*f(pO2)/(2*chi_ox), Q = 111 kJ/mol (873-1073 K) and 40 kJ/mol (623-773 K); Pilling-Bedworth 1.68 for Cu2O gives the swelling eigenstrain that produces Resch's measured +3.0% diametral and +7.8% build-direction expansion. (5) Cu2O reduction: JMAK WITH an incubation delay, alpha_red = 1 - exp(-[k_r(t-t_inc)]^q), Ea = 114.6 kJ/mol — a plain first-order Arrhenius predicts reduction far too early and hides the blistering risk. (6) Gas: d(eps*P*y_k)/dt + div(y_k*rho_g*v) = S_k, v = -(K/mu)*grad(P), K = eps^3*d^2/(180*(1-eps)^2) * (1 + b/P) Klinkenberg. Kn ~ 0.03 for a 16-18 um powder at eps = 0.5, so Darcy-plus-slip is adequate and dusty-gas is NOT needed above ~10% porosity — but the pre-percolation branch below eps_c needs free-volume diffusion through undecomposed polymer instead. (7) Energy, lumped (Bi = 2e-4 to 0.67 across every realistic case, so a conjugate 3-D thermal solve is wasted compute): rho*c_p*dT/dt = -(hA/V)(T - T_f) - (eps_r*sigma*A/V)(T^4 - T_f^4) + sum_j dH_j*r_j, with emissivity a function of oxidation state. (8) Densification, SOVS: eps_dot_in = (1/(2*eta0))*[ s/phi + (tr(sigma) - 3*sigma_s)*I/(9*psi) ], phi = (1-theta)^2, psi = (2/3)(1-theta)^3/theta, sigma_s = (3*alpha/r0)*(1-theta)^2; rho_dot = rho*tr(eps_dot_mass - eps_dot_in - 3*eps_dot_th). Keep Olevsky's seven canonical closure constants FIXED at their theoretical values (a1=1, b1=2, a2=2/3, b2=3, c2=1, a3=1, b3=2) — they are continuum-mechanics results, not free fits, and fitting them is how the identifiability problem gets manufactured. (9) SSA: dS_v/dt = -k_c*S_v^q, coupled to both gasification and reduction because both are area-proportional; SSA collapse, not pore closure at ~92%, is the real deadline. (10) Pore closure: eps_open = eps_tot*[(rho_c - rho)/(rho_c - rho_g)]^t with rho_c ~ 0.92, eps_c and t identifiable.
    - **implementation**: JAX + diffrax (Kvaerno5 for the stiff DAE, RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint for gradients), equinox for module structure. Writing the ROM in JAX is the load-bearing choice: it gives exact HMC gradients for L4, vmap-batched Monte Carlo over posterior draws for L7, and forward-mode sensitivities for L5, all for free. Method of lines, 20-50 nodes through half-thickness, Chebyshev-clustered at the surface where the reaction front lives. `rom/states.py`, `rom/kinetics.py`, `rom/oxidation.py`, `rom/transport.py`, `rom/sintering.py`, `rom/assemble.py`. Strict separation: intrinsic kinetics and transport are NEVER fitted simultaneously — that is how the compensation effect eats the predictive capability.
    - **inputs**: Priors from L0; feasibility functions from L1; furnace boundary conditions from L3; control trajectory u(t).
    - **outputs**: Full state trajectories; the six constraint margins; QoI's: residual C and O at pore closure, peak (T_part - T_furnace), peak internal pressure, final density, anisotropic shrinkage.
    - **effort**: 6-8 person-months. The core asset.
  -
    - **layer**: L3 — Furnace/Retort Reactor Model (the layer everybody omits)
    - **purpose**: Close the oxygen mass balance at the furnace scale. Every published debinding model is part-only, and that is why none of them predicts the radial carbon gradient that is the mechanistic origin of residual carbon.
    - **physics_or_math**: CSTR (or plug-flow, selectable) over the retort: V_r*d(C_k)/dt = Q_in*C_k_in - Q_out*C_k - A_part*J_k(surface). I computed the budget: full combustion of the binder in a 50 vol% Cu green body needs 1100 kg O2 per m3 of part = 34,375 mol O2/m3 = 3667 m3 STP of AIR per m3 of part. At 2 L/min synthetic air a 100 cm3 build needs >= 3.06 h at a physically impossible 100% oxygen utilisation; at a realistic few percent it is tens of hours. A loaded build platform is oxygen-starved, full stop. The consequence is a front: the exterior combusts cleanly while the interior pyrolyses and chars, and the burn front advances at 0.09-12 mm/h depending on open porosity (D_eff = D_gas*eps^1.5 Bruggeman, C_O2 from ideal gas at T). Gas-side heat transfer coefficient h is the sensitive parameter, NOT k_eff — and h is the principal design lever (see L7).
    - **implementation**: `furnace/reactor.py`, JAX, coupled to L2 by surface flux. Calibrated against an instrumented dummy load. Supports multi-part batches with a packing/shadowing factor, because a full CeraFab platform is not one coupon.
    - **inputs**: MFC setpoints, dew point, total pressure, retort free volume, part surface area and packing.
    - **outputs**: True gas composition AT THE PART SURFACE (which is not the inlet composition), oxygen utilisation, h.
    - **effort**: 2 person-months. Disproportionate payoff.
  -
    - **layer**: L4 — Bayesian Inference Engine
    - **purpose**: Turn every experiment into a posterior over the ROM's parameters, hierarchically across slurry lots, with model-form error represented honestly rather than absorbed into physical parameters.
    - **physics_or_math**: Hierarchical model: theta_lot ~ N(mu_pop, Sigma_pop) so batch-to-batch slurry variation is a random effect, not a refit. Likelihood per instrument with its own noise model (TGA balance drift as an AR(1) nuisance; LECO C/O with known repeatability; dilatometry with a pushrod compliance nuisance parameter). Model-form error via Kennedy-O'Hagan, but applied to the CONSTRAINT MARGINS rather than to raw signals — delta(x) on 'residual C at pore closure' is physically interpretable and extrapolates defensibly; delta(x) on a TGA curve is a curve-fit that will hide a missing source term. Model selection over N (number of binder pseudo-components) by Bayesian model averaging with PSIS-LOO weights, N in {2,3,4} — N is a decision, not a guess, and the number of holds in the final cycle should fall out of the number of kinetic events. Binary crack/no-crack outcomes enter through a probit likelihood on the frozen-in photopolymerisation stress latent, so FAILED PARTS ARE DATA — Roumanie's controlled F3-vs-F4 experiment (photoinitiator changed, nothing else, cracks vs no cracks) says this latent is real and possibly dominant, and it is otherwise unmeasurable.
    - **implementation**: NumPyro NUTS directly on the JAX ROM (gradients free). ArviZ for R-hat, ESS, divergence diagnostics and posterior predictive checks — CI fails if R-hat > 1.01 or divergences > 0. For the expensive 3-D likelihood, simulation-based inference via the `sbi` package (NPE/NSF) trained on L8 outputs. `infer/model.py`, `infer/likelihoods.py`, `infer/hierarchy.py`, `infer/discrepancy.py`.
    - **inputs**: All L0-curated data; the ROM; priors.
    - **outputs**: Joint posterior over ~25 parameters, marginals, pairwise correlation structure, posterior predictive with credible bands.
    - **effort**: 4 person-months.
  -
    - **layer**: L5 — Identifiability, Global Sensitivity and Parameter Triage
    - **purpose**: THE GATE. Runs on the PRIOR, before any experiment is run, and again after each update. Its output is a formal triage that decides where the budget goes. Skipping this layer is the project's most likely cause of death.
    - **physics_or_math**: Three-stage screen. (a) Structural identifiability: symbolic/numeric rank of the sensitivity Gramian. I have already demonstrated the exact degeneracy the project must confront — for SOVS free sintering, Sandia's own Eq. 31 reads xi_dot = -3*sigma_s0*xi/(4*eta0), containing only the RATIO. I verified this numerically: scaling BOTH sigma_s0 and eta0 by 100x leaves the porosity trajectory unchanged to 1.4e-16, the log-parameter Fisher information has eigenvalues (3.6e-12, 7.4e4) with condition number 2.04e16, and the null eigenvector is (-0.707, -0.707) — exactly the ratio direction. Free dilatometry at any number of heating rates cannot separate them. (b) Global sensitivity: Morris screening then Sobol' indices on the QoI's, and an active-subspace decomposition to find the low-dimensional directions that actually matter. This is where the headline result lives: in the O2-flux-limited regime d(ln t_burn)/d(ln A) -> 0 exactly, so the binder pre-exponential lands in the IRRELEVANT bucket. (c) Practical identifiability: profile likelihoods with confidence thresholds, reporting flat directions explicitly. Output is a signed triage table: IDENTIFIED (measure it), MARGINALISED (carry its prior into the credible interval), IRRELEVANT (fix at prior mean, document, move on).
    - **implementation**: SALib for Morris and Sobol'; chaospy or UQpy for sparse PCE (degree 3, hyperbolic truncation q = 0.7, least-angle regression on ~500 ROM evaluations); `athena` or a hand-rolled active subspace; profile likelihood by JAX-gradient continuation. `ident/gramian.py`, `ident/gsa.py`, `ident/profiles.py`, `ident/triage.py`. The triage table is a versioned artefact reviewed at every gate.
    - **inputs**: ROM, priors, candidate QoI list.
    - **outputs**: The triage table. Directly determines the experimental campaign.
    - **effort**: 3 person-months. Highest leverage per hour in the whole project.
  -
    - **layer**: L6 — Bayesian Optimal Experimental Design (the loop closer)
    - **purpose**: Spend scarce furnace time and coupon material on the experiment with the highest expected information gain about the parameters that L5 says matter. This is what makes the architecture a closed loop rather than a modelling exercise.
    - **physics_or_math**: Maximise EIG(d) = E_{y|d}[ KL( p(theta|y,d) || p(theta) ) ] over design d. Nested Monte Carlo is O(N^2) and biased, so use the Prior Contrastive Estimation lower bound with N = 1000 outer and L = 100 contrastive samples — O(N*L), and cheap because the ROM is vmapped in JAX. Design space is mixed: a categorical experiment type crossed with continuous settings (heating rate, isothermal T, pO2, dew point, coupon half-thickness, dwell). Critically, EIG must be computed against the DECISION, not against the parameters alone — so also evaluate the Expected Value of Information for the actual QoI, i.e. how much the chance-constrained cycle from L7 would change. An experiment that sharpens a parameter the cycle is insensitive to has high parameter-EIG and zero decision-value, and the architecture must be able to tell the difference. Worked example of what this layer buys: adding a gravity-loaded deflection observable to free dilatometry takes the SOVS Fisher condition number from 2.04e16 to 4.59e3 — thirteen decades of conditioning from ONE extra measurement channel, which no amount of extra free-sintering runs can achieve.
    - **implementation**: BoTorch for acquisition optimisation over the mixed design space (continuous relaxation plus enumeration over the categorical); custom PCE-based EIG estimator in JAX. `oed/eig.py`, `oed/design_space.py`, `oed/evoi.py`, `oed/queue.py`. Emits a ranked, costed experiment queue as a YAML artefact that the lab actually works from.
    - **inputs**: Current posterior; triage table; instrument availability and cost model.
    - **outputs**: Ranked next-experiment recommendation with predicted posterior contraction and predicted change in the recommended cycle.
    - **effort**: 3 person-months.
  -
    - **layer**: L7 — Chance-Constrained Multi-Phase Optimal Control (the cycle synthesiser)
    - **purpose**: Produce the deliverable: an optimum debinding AND sintering cycle in (temperature, dew point, H2 fraction, pO2, flow, total pressure), each with a credible interval and an explicit satisfaction probability.
    - **physics_or_math**: Multi-phase OCP with a fixed phase SEQUENCE and free switching times — six phases: (1) solvent/drying, (2) oxygen-throttled burn, (3) purge/transition, (4) reduction, (5) wet-H2 gasification hold, (6) densification. The disjunctive structure is real and must be encoded: y_O2 * y_H2 ~ 0 as a complementarity constraint, because you cannot have an oxidising and a reducing gas simultaneously. Full formulation in the dedicated field below. The single most important physical result the optimiser will exploit: I derived that in the heat-removal-limited regime the minimum oxidative debinding time is t_min = dH_c * rho_b * (V/A) / (h * dT_crit), in which TEMPERATURE DOES NOT APPEAR. Consequences — the optimal debind is near-isothermal with pO2 as the throttle rather than a temperature staircase; time scales LINEARLY with thickness, not quadratically; and the lever is the gas-side heat transfer coefficient h, so raising h from 20 to 100 W/m2K compresses a 5 mm half-thickness part from 47.7 h to 9.5 h with no added risk. That is a falsifiable, quantitative claim that contradicts the 140-156 h staircase Lithoz ships, and it is the paper.
    - **implementation**: CasADi + IPOPT (HSL MA97) with direct collocation, 3-stage Radau IIA, 150-300 finite elements. The ROM is exported from JAX to CasADi via an explicit re-implementation of the reduced state set (12-15 states, not the full 30) — keep a CI test asserting the two agree to 1e-6. Chance constraints by sparse-PCE moment propagation plus a Cantelli (one-sided Chebyshev) tightening: mean + kappa*std <= 0 with kappa = sqrt((1-eps)/eps), which is distribution-free and honest. Report BOTH the distributionally-robust kappa = 4.36 at eps = 0.05 and the Gaussian-approximation kappa = 1.645, then verify post-hoc with a 1e4-sample Monte Carlo of the posterior predictive and quote the empirical satisfaction rate. `opt/ocp.py`, `opt/phases.py`, `opt/chance.py`, `opt/verify_mc.py`. Warm-start from the CEA-LITEN DEL2 and IFAM cycles — both are industrially validated feasible points, which is free and removes the cold-start risk on a non-convex problem.
    - **inputs**: Posterior from L4; feasibility functions from L1; part geometry (V/A and half-thickness); furnace slew and MFC limits.
    - **outputs**: u*(t) with credible bands; the active-constraint trace at every instant; P[spec met]; a sensitivity report saying which posterior uncertainty is driving the conservatism.
    - **effort**: 5 person-months.
  -
    - **layer**: L8 — High-Fidelity 3-D Verification and Distortion (deliberately OUTSIDE the inference loop)
    - **purpose**: Compute what the 1-D ROM structurally cannot — gravity sag, setter friction, warpage, anisotropic distortion of a real 76 x 43 x 170 mm part — and verify the ROM. It is never calibrated against sparse data, because an 11-parameter FE model fitted to six dilatometry curves is the classic failure mode.
    - **physics_or_math**: SOVS/Olevsky poroviscoplasticity with anisotropy. Transversely isotropic sintering stress (P_lR, P_lZ) because LCM layer interfaces are real: Lithoz's own co-authored alumina data shows 18.95 (X) / 19.19 (Y) / 21.48 (Z) percent linear shrinkage, and Resch's copper shows build-axis shrinkage 2-6 points larger than in-plane. Interlayer cohesive zone with a 0.65 knockdown, matching measured green strengths of 20 (X;Z) / 17 (Y;Z) / 13 (Z;Y) MPa. Setter friction: use the MEASURED 0.87-1.31 static coefficient at temperature, not Simufact's 0.3 default — that is a 3-4x error sitting in every commercial sintering simulation. Multiplicative finite-strain split F = Fe * Fth * Fchem * Fin, with densification as a genuine INELASTIC contribution, not folded into an eigenstrain, or the residual stresses will be wrong because a sintering body is viscous.
    - **implementation**: Upstream idaholab/moose + applied-material-modeling/neml2 (MOOSE vendors NEML2 as framework/contrib/neml2 and ships scripts/update_and_rebuild_neml2.sh — this is the CI-tested path). I verified the NEML2 free-sintering regression test EXISTS at tests/regression/solid_mechanics/viscoplasticity/free_sintering and composes ThermalEigenstrain + LinearIsotropicElasticity + IsotropicMandelStress + OlevskySinteringStress + GTNYieldFunction (with the sintering-stress offset already inside the cosh term) + PerzynaPlasticFlowRate + VoceIsotropicHardening + GursonCavitation under backward-Euler ImplicitUpdate with Newton/DenseLU and a ConstantExtrapolationPredictor. Two defaults MUST be changed for copper: Perzyna exponent 2 -> 4.8 (Frost & Ashby Cu power-law creep), and particle radius 3e-4 m -> ~8e-6 m for a 16 um powder. Also settle on paper, before any other work, whether NEML2's sigma_s = 3*gamma*phi^2/r (void fraction, vanishing at full density) or the classical Olevsky rho^2 form (peaking at full density) is correct for your purpose — they differ qualitatively in exactly the late-stage regime that decides whether copper reaches target density. DO NOT build on PUMA: it is 1514 lines with zero CI, branch-pinned submodules, no sintering code whatsoever, and purely thermoelastic mechanics. Lift its kernels as patterns into your own MOOSE app. Calibrate via pyzag adjoint. `fe3d/` MOOSE app, `fe3d/materials/`, `fe3d/neml2_models/`.
    - **inputs**: ROM-calibrated material card; part CAD; setter configuration.
    - **outputs**: Distortion field, warpage, residual stress, compensated geometry; ROM closure corrections.
    - **effort**: 6 person-months, parallelisable, can start late.
  -
    - **layer**: L9 — Microstructure-to-Closure from micro-CT
    - **purpose**: Close the two transport parameters that the literature simply does not contain — there is NO measured permeability for any vat-photopolymerised green or partly-debound body, isotropic or anisotropic — by computing them from the group's own tomograms instead of guessing.
    - **physics_or_math**: Segment interrupted-debinding tomograms; extract porosity, pore-size distribution, tortuosity, and the ANISOTROPY ratio K_z/K_xy (currently a pure guess in the literature, and it is the parameter that decides delamination vs translayer cracking). Compute permeability by direct Stokes/LBM solve on the segmented volume and by pore-network extraction; compute D_eff by random-walk tortuosity. Feed back as a calibrated K(eps) and D_eff(eps) law with anisotropy, replacing the bare Kozeny-Carman assumption. Also extract S_v(rho) directly, which is the state variable governing both gasification and reduction rates.
    - **implementation**: tomopy/dxchange for reconstruction, scikit-image for segmentation, PoreSpy for morphology and tortuosity, OpenPNM for network extraction and permeability, pylbm or palabos for a direct Stokes cross-check. `micro/segment.py`, `micro/permeability.py`, `micro/closure_fit.py`.
    - **inputs**: Micro-CT stacks from interrupted-debinding coupons at 6-8 conversion levels.
    - **outputs**: K_xy(eps), K_z(eps), D_eff(eps), S_v(rho), all with uncertainty, as priors/likelihood terms for L4.
    - **effort**: 3 person-months.
- **how_unknown_binder_is_handled**: Not by discovering the chemistry. By a six-part strategy that makes the chemistry mostly irrelevant and quantifies the residue.

(1) PROVE MOST OF IT DOESN'T MATTER, FIRST. The decisive fact is regime, not chemistry. I computed the Frank-Kamenetskii number for oxidative debinding of a 50 vol% Cu acrylate green body (Q = 25 MJ/kg, rho_b = 550 kg/m3, E = 150 kJ/mol, A = 1e13, k_eff = 1 W/mK): delta = 9.6 at L = 1 mm / 250 C, 239 at 5 mm / 250 C, and 1.7e5 at 10 mm / 350 C, against a slab-critical value of 0.88. Supercritical by one to five orders of magnitude at every geometry the 2M30 can build. A reaction that far supercritical is NEVER kinetics-limited — it is limited by oxygen delivery (3667 m3 STP of air per m3 of part; burn front 0.09-12 mm/h) and by heat removal. In that regime d(ln t_burn)/d(ln A) = 0 and d(ln t_burn)/d(ln D_eff) = -1 EXACTLY. So the binder pre-exponential — the hardest thing to measure and the whole point of a DAEM campaign — has zero leverage on the part-scale answer. L5 formalises this into a triage table: IDENTIFIED / MARGINALISED / IRRELEVANT. Most published binder-kinetics effort for this material class would land in IRRELEVANT, and the architecture is built to discover that on the prior, before spending money.

(2) IDENTIFY THE SIX LUMPED INVARIANTS THAT DO MATTER. They are: phi_b, the binder VOLUME fraction (not wt% — at 50 vol% Cu the binder is only 7.6-13.1 wt%, which makes the measurement hard but does NOT make the process easy, since the gas generated per cm3 is identical to alumina); Y_C, the char carbon yield ON COPPER (the ~7 wt% argon figure is neat resin with no copper catalysis and does not transfer); k_g(800 C), the char gasification rate constant on a copper surface, which does not exist anywhere in the literature for any copper system; D_eff(eps) and the K_z/K_xy anisotropy; the oxygen-pickup coefficient (Roumanie's +8.0/+14.4/+16.8 wt% at 400/600/800 C); and eps_c, the percolation threshold with V0 = 0.03-0.10. All six are low-dimensional, all six are measurable on university kit, and all six are what L6 will select for.

(3) KILL THE COMPENSATION RIDGE BY REPARAMETERISATION, NOT BY REGULARISATION. In the raw (lnA, E) parameterisation the Jacobian columns over a 200-500 C TGA window have correlation -0.999857 — a near-perfect ridge that will make any sampler mix terribly and any optimiser report false precision. I computed the isokinetic pivot at T_iso = E/(R*lnA) = 624 K = 351 C. Reparameterise every Arrhenius term as k(T) = exp(kappa - (E/R)(1/T - 1/T_iso)) with kappa = ln k(T_iso). kappa and E are then near-orthogonal and NUTS samples them cleanly. This is a one-line change that is worth more than any amount of extra data.

(4) MARGINALISE THE REST — NEVER FIX IT AT A POINT ESTIMATE. Everything in the MARGINALISED bucket keeps its full prior and is integrated over in the posterior predictive. The cycle is then delivered as a chance constraint P[c_C(t_closure) <= 200 ppm] >= 0.95 evaluated over that predictive, so unresolved binder uncertainty shows up honestly as a wider credible band and a more conservative cycle — never as a confident wrong number. If the band is too wide to act on, L6 tells you which single experiment narrows it most.

(5) GUARD THE CONVERSION VARIABLE IN CODE. alpha = (m_t - m_0)/(m_f - m_0) is unsafe for any air-TGA of a Cu-filled sample, because copper gains +8 to +16.8 wt% while the binder inventory is only 7.6-13.1 wt% — the naive formula can return a negative binder content. L0 ships a validator that REFUSES to compute alpha from a raw air-TGA unless a matched bare-Cu-powder blank at the same d50 and ramp is linked by lot ID. The measurement protocol is the Ar->air switch in a single run: ramp in argon to 600 C to capture network scission and the residual char, then switch to air isothermally at 600 C to combust the char and close the balance. An inert-only TGA systematically under-reports binder by roughly 7% relative; an air-only TGA is uninterpretable.

(6) TREAT MODEL FORM AS UNCERTAIN TOO. The number of binder pseudo-components N is a Bayesian model-selection variable over {2,3,4} with PSIS-LOO averaging, not a guess — and the number of holds in the recommended cycle then falls out of the number of resolved kinetic events rather than out of tradition. Batch-to-batch slurry variation is a hierarchical random effect, so a new drum re-uses the population prior instead of forcing a full recalibration. Kennedy-O'Hagan discrepancy is applied to the CONSTRAINT MARGINS, not to raw TGA curves, so it stays physically interpretable and extrapolates. And the honest caveat, stated in the paper: no discrepancy term can absorb a MISSING SOURCE TERM. Reducing an 8 wt% oxygen pickup generates 22,400 mol H2O per m3 — 2432 m3 of steam per m3 of part at 1050 C, and roughly 5x the entire binder pyrolysis gas inventory. That term must be in the model form before any statistics are applied. Fix the physics, then fit the residual.
- **optimal_cycle_formulation**: A multi-phase, chance-constrained, hybrid optimal control problem solved by direct collocation.

DECISION VARIABLES. Control trajectory u(t) = (T_set(t), Q_N2(t), Q_H2(t), Q_air(t), T_dew(t), P_tot(t)) — the six things a real retort furnace with mass-flow controllers and a bubbler can actually actuate; derived quantities (pO2, pH2O/pH2, margin m(T)) are computed by L1 rather than being decision variables, so the optimiser cannot request a physically unrealisable gas. Discretised piecewise-linear on 150-300 finite elements. PLUS the phase switching times tau_1..tau_5 as free variables, PLUS the terminal time t_f. Phase sequence is FIXED at six phases (solvent/drying; oxygen-throttled burn; purge/transition; reduction; wet-H2 gasification hold; densification) because the published evidence forces that structure and a free-sequence MINLP would be gratuitous — but every duration is free, including zero, so the optimiser can delete a phase if the data says it is unnecessary.

OBJECTIVE. min t_f. Machine time and furnace hours are the group's binding resource, and using a scalarised quality-plus-time objective just hides the trade-off inside an arbitrary weight. Quality enters as constraints, not as objective terms. Run the optimiser as an epsilon-constraint sweep over the carbon spec to produce a genuine Pareto front (cycle time vs residual carbon vs P[success]) — that front, not a single cycle, is the real deliverable to the group.

CONSTRAINTS. Chance constraints over the posterior predictive: P[c_C(t_closure) <= 200 ppm] >= 0.95 (200 ppm from US5302562A, where ~1376-1550 ppm C demonstrably stops copper densifying near-dead at 59-64% TD); P[c_O(t_closure) <= 0.2 wt%] >= 0.95 (the Lin & Hwang steam-embrittlement limit, against which air-debound copper at ~2 wt% O is 10x over); P[max_t (T_part - T_furnace) <= dT_crit] >= 0.99; P[sigma_max / sigma_green(T, alpha) <= 1] >= 0.99 with an anisotropic green strength surface and a separate interlayer cohesive check. Hard deterministic constraints from L1 (no uncertainty, so no chance level): pH2O/pH2 <= boundary(T) with margin m(T) >= m_min, noting the margin collapses from 12.8x at 700 C to 1.3x at 1000 C for a fixed setpoint, so the atmosphere schedule MUST be temperature-dependent; T <= 1066 C while local c_O > 0.39 wt% (the Cu-Cu2O eutectic, 18.6 K below the pure-Cu melting point), relaxing toward 1075 C only once the oxygen state has fallen. ORDERING constraints, which are the heart of the problem and the thing a hand-tuned ramp cannot express: both the carbon and the oxide clocks must finish BEFORE rho crosses 0.92, enforced as an interior-point event constraint at t_closure. Complementarity: y_O2 * y_H2 <= epsilon_c, relaxed homotopically from 1e-2 to 1e-8. Actuator limits: |dT/dt| <= 5 K/min, dew-point slew, MFC ranges and minimum turndown.

CHANCE-CONSTRAINT HANDLING. Sparse PCE (degree 3, hyperbolic truncation q = 0.7, least-angle regression on ~500 vmapped ROM evaluations) over the ~8 triaged uncertain parameters gives analytic mean and variance of every constraint residual. Tighten with a Cantelli one-sided Chebyshev bound, mean + kappa*std <= 0 with kappa = sqrt((1-eps)/eps), which is distribution-free. Report BOTH the distributionally-robust kappa = 4.36 at eps = 0.05 and the Gaussian kappa = 1.645, and be explicit that the first is conservative by construction. Then VERIFY post-hoc with a 1e4-sample Monte Carlo of the full posterior predictive and quote the empirical satisfaction rate. Cheap in the loop, expensive in verification — never the reverse.

SOLVER. CasADi + IPOPT with HSL MA97, direct collocation on 3-stage Radau IIA. Warm-started from CEA-LITEN's DEL2 (400 C air debind) and Fraunhofer IFAM's cycle (acetone solvent debind, 24 h/120 C + 63 h/250 C air, 2 h/1050 C in 99.999% H2) — both industrially validated feasible points, free, and they remove cold-start risk on a non-convex problem. Multi-start from 20 perturbed initial guesses; report the spread, because a single local optimum presented as global is dishonest.

THE STRUCTURAL RESULT THE OPTIMISER WILL FIND, AND WHY IT IS PUBLISHABLE. In the heat-removal-limited oxidative regime the minimum debinding time is t_min = dH_c * rho_b * (V/A) / (h * dT_crit). Temperature does not appear. For a 5 mm half-thickness at h = 20 W/m2K and dT_crit = 20 K that is 47.7 h — which reproduces IFAM's 87 h cabinet debind and CEA's 5-day DEL1 cycle with zero fitted parameters. Three consequences the optimiser exploits and a human schedule-writer will not: the optimal debind is NEAR-ISOTHERMAL with pO2 as the throttle, not a temperature staircase, because ramping T buys nothing and only risks runaway; time scales LINEARLY with half-thickness, not quadratically; and the true lever is the gas-side heat transfer coefficient, so raising h from 20 to 100 W/m2K compresses that same part from 47.7 h to 9.5 h at identical risk. The optimiser also gets a genuine continuous trade: pO2 sets what fraction of the binder is removed oxidatively (clean, exothermic, heat-budget-limited) versus pyrolytically (athermal, leaves 0.25-0.39 wt% char), so at a 24 h budget for a 5 mm part only ~50% can go the oxidative route and the residue must be gasified later in the wet-H2 hold. That is a quantitative, falsifiable claim that the 140-156 h Lithoz-style staircase is not optimal, and it is the paper.

- **biggest_risks**:
  - THE MODEL FORM IS WRONG IN A WAY NO DISCREPANCY TERM CAN ABSORB. This is the dominant risk and it is not a statistical one. Reducing an 8 wt% oxygen pickup generates 22,400 mol H2O per m3 — 2432 m3 of steam per m3 of part at 1050 C, roughly 5x the entire binder pyrolysis gas inventory. If that source term is missing, no Kennedy-O'Hagan delta(x) and no embedded model-error parameter will rescue the prediction; the posterior will simply become confidently wrong, with the error absorbed into whatever physical parameter is most flexible. MITIGATION: GATE 0 is explicitly a model-FORM test run with zero fitted parameters against two anchor cycles. Fail it and stop. Never calibrate past a form failure.
  - THE OED LOOP IS DECORATIVE. Bayesian optimal experimental design is easy to implement and easy to ignore, and the failure mode is that the lab runs the experiments it already planned while the EIG module produces unread YAML. It is also genuinely possible that EIG is dominated by one or two experiments and the rest of the machinery earns nothing. MITIGATION: GATE 2 requires a completed loop turn with predicted-vs-achieved posterior contraction agreeing within 3x, and L6 computes decision-value (EVOI on the actual cycle) not just parameter-EIG — an experiment that sharpens an irrelevant parameter must score zero, visibly.
  - MY CENTRAL CLAIM COULD BE WRONG AT THE EDGES. The flux-limited argument rests on generic acrylate values (Q = 25 MJ/kg, E = 150 kJ/mol, A = 1e13, k_eff = 1 W/mK). Frank-Kamenetskii delta is supercritical by 1-5 orders, so the conclusion is robust to large errors in any one of them — but k_eff is the weak link, currently bounded only between 0.8 W/mK (Maxwell-Eucken, polymer continuous) and ~130 W/mK (Bruggeman), a two-order spread. At the Bruggeman bound delta drops by two decades and the regime could shift near the thin end. MITIGATION: laser-flash or hot-disk on cured green coupons in month 1; until then run the debinding model at BOTH bounds and report the envelope rather than a trajectory.
  - SPARSE DATA MEETS A FLEXIBLE MODEL AND THE POSTERIOR IS HONEST BUT USELESS. The credible interval on residual carbon may be so wide that the chance-constrained cycle collapses to something absurdly conservative — the Cantelli tightening at eps = 0.05 uses kappa = 4.36, which is deliberately pessimistic. The project would then have produced a rigorous recommendation nobody can act on. MITIGATION: report both the distribution-free and the Gaussian tightening; use the Pareto front so the group chooses its own risk level explicitly; and let L6 identify which single posterior direction is driving the conservatism so it can be attacked directly.
  - THE BINDER CHANGES UNDER US. It is an internal R&D slurry with no datasheet and no change control. Lithoz may reformulate between drums, and native oxide on the powder grows as d_ox = 3.66*t^0.266 nm with t in days, so storage history alone shifts the oxygen inventory. A point-calibrated model would silently go stale. MITIGATION: the hierarchical lot structure is in the architecture from day one precisely for this; a new drum re-uses the population prior and re-qualifies on one TGA plus one dilatometry run rather than a full campaign. Also: ask Lithoz under NDA for three non-disclosing numbers — organic volume fraction, powder d50 and LECO oxygen, and photoinitiator phosphorus in ppm-of-slurry. An NDA is cheaper than the experimental matrix needed to infer them.
  - THE LITERATURE BASE IS SHAKIER THAN IT LOOKS. Material flagged MAJOR-unverified in the brief includes essentially all of the Metals 2024, 14, 1268 debinding content, every number attributed to the Ott dissertation (densification onset, peak rate, the rho(t) parameter set, the native-oxide law), and the Abouaf calibration paragraph which rests on a mis-cited arXiv preprint that is actually about hot isostatic pressing. Several numbers the project would naturally seed from are single-sourced and unchecked. MITIGATION: a provenance field on every entry in priors.yaml with a verified/unverified flag; unverified values get deliberately wide priors; someone with institutional access opens the Metals and Ott PDFs in month 1 before anything enters an input file.
  - THE FE3D LAYER EATS THE PROJECT. MOOSE/NEML2 is powerful and it is a tar pit — six months can vanish into build systems and constitutive debugging while the inference loop, which is where the actual contribution lives, stalls. NEML2's sintering stress also uses sigma_s = 3*gamma*phi^2/r (void fraction, vanishing at full density) rather than the classical rho^2 form, and the two differ qualitatively in exactly the late-stage regime that decides whether copper reaches target density. MITIGATION: L8 is explicitly outside the inference loop and starts late (month 14); the ROM is the deliverable and the FE model is a verification and distortion tool; settle the sintering-stress form on paper before writing any code; and do NOT build on PUMA (1514 lines, zero CI, branch-pinned submodules, no sintering code, thermoelastic-only mechanics) — lift its kernels as patterns into a vanilla MOOSE app.
  - FROZEN-IN PHOTOPOLYMERISATION STRESS IS REAL, POSSIBLY DOMINANT, AND ESSENTIALLY UNMEASURABLE. Roumanie's controlled experiment (F3 vs F4, photoinitiator changed and nothing else, cracks vs no cracks) points at it directly, and no measurement technique or numerical model for it was found anywhere in the copper literature. If it dominates cracking, a thermal-mechanical failure criterion will mispredict regardless of how well everything else is calibrated. MITIGATION: carry it as a latent variable informed by binary crack/no-crack outcomes through a probit likelihood, so failed parts become data rather than anecdotes; and treat exposure dose and overlap as experimental factors in the shrinkage-bar campaign so the latent has something to correlate with.
  - HYDROGEN EMBRITTLEMENT ARRIVES THROUGH A ROUTE THE OPTIMISER CANNOT SEE. Air-debound copper sits at roughly 2 wt% O, about 10x over the Lin & Hwang 0.2 mass% limit, and copper's water-gas shift activity generates H2 in situ inside pores even when the inlet gas is nominally dry. A cycle that is safe by inlet composition can be unsafe at the pore scale. MITIGATION: L1 models WGS as a fast surface equilibrium rather than assuming frozen gas chemistry; blistering is modelled as creep-driven void growth competing with densification in the same solver, not as a post-hoc check; and the ordering constraint (oxide reduction complete before rho crosses 0.92) is a hard event constraint, not a soft penalty.
- **why_this_beats_alternatives**: The obvious alternative is physics-fidelity-first: build the best possible 3-D multiphysics model in MOOSE, calibrate it against whatever data the group can afford, and run a parameter sweep for the cycle. That approach fails here for a reason I can state quantitatively rather than rhetorically. It would spend its calibration budget on binder decomposition kinetics — the DAEM campaign, the multi-rate TGA matrix, the isoconversional stack — and those parameters have provably zero leverage on the part-scale answer, because Frank-Kamenetskii delta is 9.6 to 1.7e5 against a critical value of 0.88, which puts the whole process in an oxygen-flux-limited regime where d(ln t_burn)/d(ln A) = 0 exactly. It would then attempt to fit an 11-parameter SOVS model to free dilatometry, where sigma_s0 and eta0 are EXACTLY structurally non-identifiable: I measured the Fisher condition number at 2.04e16 with null direction (-0.707, -0.707), and confirmed that scaling both by 100x changes nothing to 1.4e-16. A sweep-based optimiser cannot express the constraint that actually matters — that carbon and oxygen must both clear BEFORE relative density reaches 0.92 — because that is a constraint on the ORDER of events, which only an optimal-control formulation with an interior-point event constraint can state. And it would deliver a single deterministic cycle with no credible interval from a model whose binder is unknown, which is not a research result, it is a guess with a mesh.

A second alternative is empirical: copy the CEA-LITEN or Fraunhofer IFAM recipe, run a design of experiments, and tune. That is genuinely sensible as a starting point — which is why CUPOLA runs both anchor cycles verbatim in phase 0 and warm-starts the optimiser from them. But it cannot extrapolate across thickness, and thickness is exactly where the failure lives: Roumanie's own data shows 0.022 wt% O in a thin gear and 0.062 wt% in a thick cylinder under an identical cycle. A DoE on 5 mm coupons tells you nothing safe about a 20 mm section, and the CeraFab Multi 2M30 builds to 170 mm. CUPOLA's governing law t_min = dH_c*rho_b*(V/A)/(h*dT_crit) is a scaling relation, so it transfers, and the registered blind thickness-transfer test in phase 4 puts that claim at risk on purpose.

What the inverse-first framing buys that neither alternative can. First, it discovers that the binder is largely irrelevant BEFORE spending the budget on it, by running global sensitivity and identifiability on the prior in months 4-7 rather than after a year of TGA. Second, it turns the sparse, expensive experiment problem from a constraint into a decision variable: EIG selects the embedded-thermocouple coupon and the dual-axis gravity-loaded dilatometry over the more familiar experiments, and the second of those alone improves the SOVS conditioning by thirteen decades — an improvement no quantity of additional free-sintering runs can buy, at any budget. Third, the reduced-order model is not a compromise, it is the enabling choice: a sub-second JAX DAE is what makes 1e5 posterior samples, PCE-based chance constraints and vmapped EIG affordable at all, and a 3-D PDE in the inference loop would make every one of those impossible. Fidelity is not free, and here it would cost the entire method.

Finally, the publication case is stronger. The oxygen-in/oxygen-out coupled inventory for copper VPP is unpublished; the master sintering curve for pure copper appears genuinely unpublished; char gasification rate constants on copper do not exist anywhere; there is no measured permeability for ANY vat-photopolymerised body; and no copper SOVS material card exists — every published set is ZnO, alumina, silica, porcelain, 316L or 17-4PH. A fidelity-first project would produce one more simulation paper in a crowded genre. This one produces a falsifiable scaling law that says the 140-156 h Lithoz-style staircase is not optimal and that the lever is the gas-side heat transfer coefficient rather than the temperature programme, plus a methodology for thermal post-processing of any proprietary feedstock. The second contribution outlives the copper.
