- **actionable_for_architecture**:
  - INVERT THE DEBINDING CONSTRAINT HIERARCHY. Do not build the debinding optimiser around gas pressure. Build it around a lumped thermal-runaway model: dT_part/dt = [q_rxn(T_part, alpha, pO2) - h*A/V*(T_part - T_furnace)]/(rho*cp), with q_rxn from the DAEM kinetics multiplied by the heat of combustion. Constrain T_part - T_furnace <= dT_crit. Keep the Darcy/Lombardo pressure module in the code but run it as a DIAGNOSTIC that reports constraint activity; demonstrating it is inactive is itself a result. The controls are T_furnace(t) AND inlet pO2(t).
  - MAKE THE SOLVER MULTI-SCALE IN A SPECIFIC WAY: TGA-scale (kinetics only, no transport, no exotherm) -> coupon-scale (exotherm on, transport on) -> part-scale (plus furnace oxygen mass balance). Fit kinetics only at TGA scale; never fit exotherm or transport parameters against TGA data, because those mechanisms are structurally absent there. This staging is what makes the CRTA out-of-sample test meaningful rather than circular.
  - ADD A FURNACE-SCALE REACTOR MASS BALANCE — this is missing from every model in the recon's literature. At 990 kg O2 per m3 of green body and 0.009 g O2/s from a 2 L/min air flow, a loaded build platform is oxygen-starved. Model the retort as a CSTR (or plug flow) with inlet composition, and couple part-surface consumption to the bulk gas. An oxygen-starved interior pyrolyses (leaving carbon) while the exterior combusts (clean), which produces a radial CARBON GRADIENT — that is the mechanistic origin of the residual-carbon problem and it cannot be captured by a part-only model.
  - IMPLEMENT THE SEQUENCING CONSTRAINT AS A HARD COUPLING BETWEEN THE TWO PHYSICS BLOCKS. Carry a residual-oxygen field and a residual-carbon field from the end of debinding into the sintering solve, and enforce that both reach spec BEFORE the relative density crosses the open-to-closed porosity transition (rho ~ 0.90-0.95). The penalty for violating it is quantified: 95-236 part volumes of trapped steam. This single coupling is the paper. It implies a LOW-TEMPERATURE REDUCTION DWELL (roughly 400-700 C in dry H2 to strip oxide, then a humidified window above ~800 C to gasify carbon) before the densification ramp — a cycle shape that no published copper LMM work uses.
  - BUILD THE ATMOSPHERE AS A SECOND CONTROL VARIABLE, PARAMETERISED BY DEWPOINT, NOT pO2. Dewpoint is what a furnace actually sets and what a humidifier actually controls. Provide the Magnus conversion in the input deck. Because the Cu2O upper bound is unreachable, the dewpoint schedule is optimised purely against carbon-removal rate versus the pore-closure clock — a clean, well-posed, and novel sub-problem.
  - USE A LUMPED-CAPACITANCE THERMAL MODEL FOR THE PART AND SPEND THE EFFORT ON h INSTEAD. Bi = 2e-4 to 0.67 across every realistic case, so a conjugate 3D heat-transfer solve inside the part is wasted computation. The sensitive parameter is the furnace heat-transfer coefficient h, which should be calibrated from a thermocouple embedded in a dummy part. This is a large and easy saving in solver complexity.
  - MEASURE THE GREEN THERMAL CONDUCTIVITY EARLY — it is the input with the widest current uncertainty (0.93 to 130 W/m.K, two orders of magnitude, from the Maxwell-Eucken and Bruggeman bounds). Laser flash or hot-disk on cured green coupons. Until it is measured, run the whole debinding model at both bounds and report the envelope rather than a single trajectory.
  - SEED THE SINTERING MODEL WITH THE COMPUTED COPPER CONSTANTS AND TREAT DILATOMETRY AS CALIBRATION, NOT DISCOVERY. Starting values: sigma_s0 = 1.0-5.6 MPa depending on r0, eta0 = 1.1e10 / 5.4e9 / 2.8e9 Pa.s at 950 / 1000 / 1050 C for 5 um powder, predicted free-sintering rate 11-46 %/h. Keep the Olevsky shape functions (phi = rho^2, psi = (2/3)rho^3/(1-rho), sigma_s = sigma_s0*rho^2) but discard the ZnO viscosity constants a4/b4/c4 = 517/-1066/564 GPa.s entirely — use them only as the functional template. If the first dilatometry run disagrees with 11-46 %/h by more than ~3x, suspect the measurement (push-rod load, oxide, residual carbon) before suspecting the model.
  - RUN LOADED (SINTER-FORGE) DILATOMETRY AT 3+ STRESS LEVELS AS A FIRST-CLASS EXPERIMENT, NOT AN AFTERTHOUGHT. It does double duty: it tests the SOVS linear-viscosity assumption at 0.9-0.95 Tm (where it is most suspect), AND it is exactly the zero-load extrapolation that removes the push-rod contact-pressure artefact. One experimental campaign answers two of the open questions. The Sandia report's sinter-forge verification case gives you a ready-made numerical benchmark.
  - STRUCTURE THE CODE SO THE OPTIMISER IS CONSTRAINT-AGNOSTIC. Given that the recon's assumed active constraint (pressure) is probably inactive and the real one (exotherm) was not in the original list, the architecture must allow constraints to be registered, activated and reported dynamically. Follow Lombardo and Retzloff's FE + process-control pattern: a servo loop that drives T_furnace to hold the MOST ACTIVE constraint at its limit, with the active-set identity logged over time. The plot of which constraint is active when is a publishable figure in itself.
  - VALIDATE STATISTICALLY AND PRE-REGISTER THE HOLD-OUT. The recon is right about this and it is the cheapest credibility win available: fit kinetics on 1/2/5/10/20 K/min, then predict a CRTA or stepwise-isothermal run never used in the fit, and report RMSE on that prediction. Shende and Lombardo's +/-15% result means in-sample agreement proves nothing. Decide and record the hold-out programme BEFORE fitting.
  - ESTABLISH THE REPEATABILITY FLOOR BEFORE ATTEMPTING COMPENSATION. Build 3-5 nominally identical parts, scan them, and compute the run-to-run deviation-map scatter. Displacement-reversal iteration converges geometrically and will appear to stall at that floor; without it you cannot distinguish algorithm convergence from process noise, and any claimed iteration count is meaningless. Use the mf3 sintered-copper precedent (Montes-Ramirez et al. 2024) for the pipeline and Ghantasala/Bouvard (Acta Mater 2025) for the FE side.
  - SCOPE THE GEOMETRY TO THE MACHINE. 76 x 43 x 170 mm envelope, 35 um lateral, 10-100 um layers. Because the exotherm self-heating scales LINEARLY with half-thickness (27 K at 1 mm, 138 K at 5 mm, 275 K at 10 mm at 0.4 C/min), the deliverable should not be 'the optimum cycle' but a THICKNESS-PARAMETERISED CYCLE FAMILY, t_f and T(t) as functions of the maximum inscribed half-thickness of the part. That is far more useful to the group, it matches the Euro PM2025 stainless-steel finding that thickness is the controlling variable, and it is a better paper.
  - DEFER THE MULTI-MATERIAL CO-SINTERING WORK. Copper sinters at 950-1070 C while Lithoz ceramics sinter at 1400-1600 C, so a Cu/ceramic interface is a fully-constrained-sintering problem with the entire sintering stress (1-5.6 MPa) reacted at the interface, not a mild differential-shrinkage problem. It is a second paper, and it should not start until the single-material copper cycle is validated.
