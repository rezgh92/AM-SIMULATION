- **name**: CUPOLA — Coupled Unbinding, Pyrolysis, Oxidation & Lithography-Aware sintering. A hierarchical multiscale continuum framework with an oxygen-ledger core and a multiphase hybrid optimal-control cycle generator, built on MOOSE + NEML2.
- **thesis**: The core bet: the controlling physics of copper LCM thermal post-processing is neither binder pyrolysis kinetics nor Darcy gas transport — it is a coupled OXYGEN LEDGER run under a moving, oxygen-flux-limited combustion front. Roumanie's screen is decisive: only an oxidising debind reaches powder-level carbon (0.019 wt% in air vs 0.25 in H2, 0.394 in Ar, ~0.36 in vacuum/600 ppm O2/5% O2 — a 20x swing driven purely by atmosphere while T, dwell and pH2 did essentially nothing). So oxygen must be deliberately IMPORTED to gasify a charring acrylate, and that same oxygen (up to +8 wt% on the metal at 400 C = 5.0 mol O/kg Cu = 112 L STP steam/kg, verified here) must be completely EXPORTED as steam before pore closure, or the part blisters. Every other module exists to resolve the timing and spatial distribution of that O-in/O-out loop. Three consequences drive the whole design. (1) The decision variable is (T(t), pO2(t) or pH2O/pH2(t), P(t), flow(t)) plus atmosphere-switching times — a hybrid multiphase optimal-control problem — because a pure T(t) optimiser provably cannot discover the air-then-hydrogen structure that both independent groups converged on. (2) The binding constraint is an ORDERING constraint — residual C and O must reach spec before rho crosses the open->closed porosity transition — which is exactly what makes this optimal control rather than curve-fitting, and what gives the optimiser a reason to deliberately SLOW the approach to 800-900 C. (3) I verified that throttling inlet pO2 throttles the available exotherm power LINEARLY (137 W at air, 32.7 W at 1% O2, ~6.5 W at 0.2%, for 10 L/min) while temperature does so exponentially and unstably — so in the pyrolysis window the correct control is a near-isothermal hold with a rising pO2 ramp, not a rising T ramp. That is the opposite shape to Lithoz's 156 h staircase and it is the publishable claim. We accept a ~26-month build because the alternative — fitting a schedule to coupons — cannot extrapolate in section thickness, and thickness is precisely what changes between a 3 mm test disc and a real part (Roumanie's own data: 0.022 wt% O in a thin gear vs 0.062 in a thick cylinder under an IDENTICAL cycle).
- **thesis_verification_note**: All numbers in this architecture that are marked "verified here" were recomputed independently during this design session; four of them contradict the supplied research brief and those contradictions are called out explicitly in the relevant layers and risks.

    - **layer**: L1 — Mesoscale Parameter Foundry (DEM + LBM + phase-field)
    - **purpose**: Supply exactly the three constitutive quantities that are PROVABLY non-identifiable from any experiment this group can run. This is the justification for multiscale — not rigour for its own sake, but three specific numbers that cannot be obtained otherwise.
    - **physics_or_math**: (a) DEM layer-wise packing: sample the measured PSD, simulate doctor-blade spreading of 25-100 um layers with a cure step between, to reproduce the INTER-LAYER packing defect that Maniere/Marinel showed is the physical origin of shrinkage anisotropy in vat-photopolymerised parts. Output: packing fraction, coordination number Z(z) modulated at the layer period, inter-layer porosity excess. (b) LBM/Stokes on the voxelised pack -> FULL permeability tensor K_ij(eps) and hence K_z/K_xy. No direct permeability measurement exists for ANY vat-photopolymerised green body, isotropic or anisotropic; K_z/K_xy is currently a pure guess and it is the parameter that decides delamination vs translayer cracking. Calibrate the Kozeny-Carman prefactor against the LBM result rather than assuming k0=5. (c) Phase-field multi-grain sintering (Kazaryan/Wang type, free energy with rigid-body motion + surface and GB diffusion) on a representative volume, with Frost & Ashby Cu data read from the primary table: Omega=1.18e-29 m3, b=2.56e-10 m, T_M=1356 K, mu_0=42.1 GPa with (T_M/mu_0)(dmu/dT)=-0.54, D_0v=2.0e-5 m2/s Q_v=197 kJ/mol, delta*D_0b=5.0e-15 m3/s Q_b=104 kJ/mol (NB Frost & Ashby footnote (h) flags the boundary data as partly ESTIMATED via delta*D_0B=b*D_0v and Q_B=0.6*Q_v — carry that as an uncertainty, not a fact), a_c*D_0c=1.0e-24 m4/s Q_c=117 kJ/mol, n=4.8, A=7.4e5. Apply separate DEVIATORIC and VOLUMETRIC strain rates to the RVE and measure the stress response -> the shear/bulk viscosity ratio, i.e. Olevsky's phi(theta) and psi(theta). Also compute sigma_s(rho) DIRECTLY from the free energy, which settles the phi^2-vs-rho^2 question numerically and independently of the conflicting literature. Also fit Q_s in [0.4,0.6]Q_v (79-118 kJ/mol) against simulated surface-area loss, since Swinkels & Ashby Cu surface-diffusion constants are unretrievable and must never be guessed into the code.

  -
    - **layer**: L2 — 0-D Chemistry & Densification Kernel (NEML2)
    - **purpose**: The scientific heart and the place 90% of calibration happens. Deliberately split from the 3-D solver along the IDENTIFIABILITY seam, not the physics seam, because the trace of the SOVS strain rate contains only (eta0, psi, sigma_s) and never phi — so bulk behaviour can and must be calibrated in 0-D and frozen before any 3-D fitting is attempted.
    - **physics_or_math**: State vector — and the single most important design decision is that this is a VECTOR, not a scalar 'binder burnout': (alpha_1..alpha_N binder pseudo-components; c_char; S_liq mobile/fugitive organic saturation; chi_ox Cu oxidation extent; chi_red Cu2O reduction extent; gas composition y_H2,y_H2O,y_CO,y_CO2,y_CH4,y_O2,y_HC and pressure p; rho_rel; G grain size; SSA; [O]_metal; [C]_metal). Governing laws: (1) Binder — Sestak-Berggren IPR as in L0. (2) SOLVENT — evaporation-diffusion with constant-rate then falling-rate period, mdot = h_m a (p_sat(T)-p_v)/(RT), p_sat by Antoine. NOT an Arrhenius solid-state decomposition. This matters enormously: Lithoz's own published alumina cycle spends 106 of 156 h at or below 205 C, i.e. 68% of the entire schedule is in this regime, so it is where the largest compression win lives, and it is the module a conventional pyrolysis model does not have at all. (3) Cu oxidation — parabolic dchi/dt = k_p(T,pO2)/chi with TWO activation energies, Q=40 kJ/mol over 623-773 K and Q=111 kJ/mol over 873-1073 K (the low-T drop is GB diffusion of Cu along fine columnar Cu2O grains plus a non-protective CuO whisker layer). Anchored on three TGA points: +8.0/+14.4/+16.8 wt% at 400/600/800 C, 4 h, 1 K/min. I verified +8 wt% O = 5.0 mol O/kg Cu = 63.5% conversion to Cu2O = +43.2 vol% = +12.72% LINEAR swelling of the metal phase at PBR 1.68. (4) Cu2O reduction — JMAK/Avrami WITH AN EXPLICIT NUCLEATION INDUCTION TIME tau_inc(T,pH2), NOT first-order Arrhenius: Ea ~ 114.6 kJ/mol for Cu2O, ~60.7 for CuO, ~72 for propagation once metallic fronts exist. Using a plain Arrhenius rate here predicts reduction far too early and thereby HIDES the blistering risk — the single most dangerous model-form error available in this project. (5) Char gasification C + H2O -> CO + H2 by Random Pore Model, rate proportional to SSA, Ea prior 176-222 kJ/mol, product-inhibited by pH2. I recomputed the equilibrium constant: 2.3e-2 (500 C), 0.233 (600 C), 1.575 (700 C), 7.45 (800 C), 27.1 (900 C), 80.2 (1000 C) — K crosses unity at ~660 C, which is the thermodynamic origin of copper's 'carbon gap' and of the industrial 785 C burnout hold. Because the rate goes as pH2O and is inhibited by pH2, the optimum is HIGH pH2O and LOW pH2 — superheated steam with ppm-level H2, i.e. the IBM recipe (H2/H2O = 1e-4 at 700-785 C), which is roughly 1e4 times LESS reducing than a naive 'humidify freely' instruction. (6) Gas-phase — water-gas shift CO + H2O <-> CO2 + H2 treated as a FAST Cu-catalysed surface reaction; copper is an excellent WGS catalyst, so internal pCO is capped far below its ~1e5 atm equilibrium value and an in-situ H2 source is created that feeds blistering. Frozen gas-phase chemistry is not admissible. (7) Ellingham constraint module computing pO2 from the actual (pH2O,pH2,pCO,pCO2). I recomputed the Cu/Cu2O boundary from dG(4Cu+O2=2Cu2O) = -333400 + 141.3T and dG(2H2+O2=2H2O) = -492900 + 109.6T: max pH2O/pH2 = 4.29e9 (200 C), 1.25e8 (300), 1.04e7 (400), 1.65e6 (500), 3.97e5 (600), 1.29e5 (700), 5.13e4 (800), 2.39e4 (900), 1.26e4 (1000), 8.28e3 (1075) — reproducing the brief's table to within 7%. The reducing-side margin of a FIXED H2/H2O=1e-4 setpoint collapses from ~12.8x at 700 C to ~1.3x at 1000 C, so a constant setpoint is unsafe above ~900 C and the margin m(T) must itself be a scheduled control. (8) SSA evolution by surface-diffusion coarsening plus densification — carried as a first-class state because BOTH gasification and oxide reduction are area-proportional, and SSA collapses long before open porosity closes. (9) Grain growth G^n - G0^n = k0 t exp(-Q/RT) with porosity drag. (10) Densification by SOVS with sigma_s and eta0 (see L4 for the blocking defect).
    - **implementation**: cupola-kernel/ as NEML2 Python models composed into a ComposedModel, integrated by ImplicitUpdate + Newton/DenseLU with a ConstantExtrapolationPredictor, calibrated by pyzag ADJOINT against multi-rate TGA — GPU batching and gradients come free. I verified by direct inspection of the cloned repo that NEML2 already ships much of this and the brief UNDERSTATED it: neml2/models/chemical_reactions/ contains AvramiErofeevNucleation (f = k(1-a)(-ln(1-a))^n — exactly the JMAK form needed for Cu2O reduction), ContractingGeometry (f = k(1-a)^n, shrinking core), DiffusionLimitedReaction, ReactionMechanism (base class), EffectiveVolume, CylindricalChannelGeometry; neml2/models/porous_flow/ contains KozenyCarmanPermeability, PowerLaw/ExponentialLaw permeability, Brooks-Corey and van Genuchten capillary pressure, EffectiveSaturation; neml2/models/finite_volume/ contains upwinded advective flux, gradient and cell-edge interpolation operators. NEML2 is alive and CI-tested (10 workflows, HEAD 8149c30 dated 2026-09-18). MUST-WRITE models, each ~60-120 lines of PyTorch following the existing file patterns: SestakBerggrenMechanism (absent — this is the single most important gap), CuOxidationParabolic (dual-Q), Cu2OReductionJMAK (adds the induction time Avrami lacks), CharGasificationRPM, SolventEvaporation, EllinghamMargin, WaterGasShift, SSAEvolution, and CuSinteringStress (see L4).
    - **inputs**: L0 posterior ensemble; L1 tabulated constitutive functions; TGA/DSC/dilatometry data
    - **outputs**: Calibrated 0-D kernel + frozen densification kernel; adjoint gradients for L7
    - **effort**: 4 months, 1 postdoc (Python/PyTorch, not MOOSE C++ — staff accordingly)

    - **layer**: L3 — Part-Scale Reactive Transport & Thermal (MOOSE)
    - **purpose**: Resolve the spatial structure that 0-D cannot: the oxygen combustion front, the radial carbon gradient, the internal thermal excursion, and the thickness dependence that is the entire justification for the physics-first bet.
    - **physics_or_math**: Energy: (rho c)_eff dT/dt = div(k_eff grad T) + sum_j dH_j r_j, with k_eff an EXPLICIT function of binder conversion, not just density — it spans ~0.8-1.1 W/m.K in the green state (Maxwell-Eucken with the POLYMER as continuous phase at 50-60 vol% Cu) through tens of W/m.K in the brown skeleton to ~390 W/m.K dense. The phase inversion is the whole point, and the worst thermal gradients occur exactly during pyrolysis when k is lowest AND the exotherm is largest. IMPORTANT CORRECTION I established numerically, contradicting the brief: Biot is NOT << 1 in all cases. With h=20 W/m2.K, Bi = 0.044 at L=2 mm green but 0.478 at L=21.5 mm green (and 21.5 mm is exactly the half-envelope of the machine's 43 mm Y axis). So a lumped-capacitance part model is INVALID during debinding for thick sections and valid only in the brown/sintering state (Bi = 2.7e-3 at L=21.5 mm, k=160). The thermal solve must be spatially resolved during debinding and may be collapsed during sintering — the opposite of the usual assumption. Gas transport: d(eps rho_g y_k)/dt + div(y_k rho_g u - eps rho_g D_k^eff grad y_k) = omega_k with Darcy + Klinkenberg slip, u = -(K/mu)(1 + b/p) grad p, K from the L1 tensor. Full dusty-gas is NOT needed at atmospheric pressure — with d50 = 16-18 um and eps ~ 0.5 the hydraulic pore radius is ~5 um, Kn ~ 0.03 and b/p ~ 0.1 — but DGM ships behind a switch for the low-conversion and sub-ambient (Roumanie ran H2 at 50-600 mbar) branches. SECOND IMPORTANT CORRECTION, also contradicting the brief: the internal-pressure constraint is NOT 'nearly slack'. I computed the Darcy centre overpressure for a 5 mm half-thickness, 16 um powder, all binder converting over a 150 K-wide event: at eps=0.15 and 0.4 C/min it is 269 bar, against a green strength of only 100-230 bar — i.e. ACTIVE. It falls to 22.8 bar at eps=0.30 and 4.2 bar at eps=0.45. The crossover is eps ~ 0.25-0.30, not the 0.05-0.10 the brief claims. The resolution is subtle and is a genuine finding: the constraint is active in the PYROLYTIC (inert or oxygen-starved-interior) regime and is rendered inactive in air precisely BECAUSE oxygen diffusion rate-limits the local reaction. Choosing an oxidising atmosphere therefore solves the carbon problem AND self-suppresses the pressure spike — at the price of the exotherm and the 12.7% oxidation swelling. That three-way trade is the design space. Oxygen front: Damkohler number Da = kL^2/D_eff >> 1 in air gives a sharp front, so the exterior combusts clean while the oxygen-starved interior pyrolyses and chars — the mechanistic origin of the residual-carbon problem, invisible to any 0-D or well-mixed model. Thermal runaway as a first-class constraint, not a post-hoc check: Frank-Kamenetskii delta = Q rho A E L^2/(k_eff R T^2) exp(-E/RT) < 0.88 for a slab, plus the quasi-steady part-minus-furnace excess dT ~ q_gen L/h which I verified scales LINEARLY with half-thickness (at 0.4 C/min, h=20: 37 K at L=1 mm, 184 K at 5 mm, 368 K at 10 mm, 791 K at 21.5 mm). That linear-in-L scaling is why section thickness is the controlling debinding variable and why a coupon-fitted schedule destroys a real part.
    - **implementation**: cupola-moose/, a standard MOOSE app on UPSTREAM idaholab/moose (which vendors NEML2 at framework/contrib/neml2 and ships scripts/update_and_rebuild_neml2.sh — a first-class CI-tested path). Do NOT build on PUMA: I confirmed by cloning it that PUMA has zero CI (no .github directory at all), pins its submodules by BRANCH to a personal MOOSE fork rather than by tag, has only 45 source files, and contains NO sintering code whatsoever (grep -ril sinter matches exactly one file: README.md). Instead PORT its patterns — PumaCoupledTimeDerivative, PumaCoupledDiffusion, PumaCoupledDarcyFlow, CoupledL2Projection, CoupledMaterialSource, CoupledDirichletBC — which is days of work on ~1,500 lines, and gains reproducibility. Reuse MOOSE's porous_flow and chemical_reactions modules where they fit; drive all constitutive response through NEML2.
    - **inputs**: L2 kernel; L1 K_ij(eps); geometry from the sliced build; L5 boundary gas state
    - **outputs**: Fields of T, p, y_k, alpha, chi_ox, [C], [O], eps; crack-risk indicators; thickness-resolved predictions
    - **effort**: 5 months, 1 research engineer (MOOSE C++)

    - **layer**: L4 — Poro-Viscoplastic Mechanics, Anisotropy & Distortion
    - **purpose**: Predict shrinkage, anisotropic distortion, gravity sag, setter drag, and the two crack modes separately. Contains the project's single blocking software defect.
    - **physics_or_math**: Finite-strain multiplicative split F = F_e . F_th . F_ox . F_vp . F_chem. F_ox is the term NO ceramic debinding model has and it DOMINATES the dimensional budget: Resch measured +3.0 +/- 0.3% diametral and +7.8 +/- 0.6% build-direction EXPANSION during air debinding (a 2.6x anisotropy), consistent with my computed +12.7% linear swelling at full +8 wt% O. Then it contracts again on reduction. Constitutive: poro-viscoplastic with two branches behind a switch. Default is SOVS/Olevsky LINEAR VISCOUS (N=1), justified on stress scale — sintering driving stress sigma_s0 = 3 alpha/r0 is only 0.4-2.7 MPa for alpha ~ 1.4-1.8 J/m2 and r0 = 2-10 um, and gravity in a 10 mm part is ~0.9 kPa, both one to four orders below the power-law transition; SPS data on Cu gives pressure exponent n=1 at 10-17 MPa. The alternative branch is GTN/Perzyna with n = 4.8 from Frost & Ashby for Cu (NOT the n=2 placeholder sitting in NEML2's own free_sintering test deck) for any future pressure-assisted work. THE BLOCKING DEFECT, which I verified by reading the source: NEML2's OlevskySinteringStress implements sigma_s = 3*gamma*phi^2/r where phi is the VOID FRACTION, so the sintering stress vanishes at full density. The classical Olevsky form is sigma_s = (3 alpha/r0) rho^2 = (3 alpha/r0)(1-theta)^2, which is MAXIMAL at full density. I computed the gap for gamma=1.5 J/m2, r=8 um: at rho=0.92 NEML2 gives 0.0036 MPa vs 0.4761 MPa classical — 132x too small; at rho=0.95, 361x; at rho=0.98, 2400x. NEML2's form will therefore stall densification precisely in the 92->99% window that decides whether this project succeeds or produces another 90%-dense part. This must be resolved BEFORE anything else is built. Anisotropy: transversely isotropic throughout, with separate in-plane and build-direction sintering stresses (P_lR, P_lZ) and separate viscosities. Anchors: sintered shrinkage 15.5-16.8% in-plane vs 17.9-21.7% build axis for DLP copper; Lithoz alumina 18.95/19.19/21.48% (Z shrinks ~2.4 points more). Build direction is a state variable from day one. Interlayer cohesive zone with separate normal and shear strengths — green bending strength is 20 (X;Z), 17 (Y;Z), 13 (Z;Y) MPa at 52 vol%, a 0.65 interlaminar knockdown, and rises 10 -> 23 MPa as cure overlap goes 0.75 -> 2. Report DELAMINATION and TRANSLAYER cracking as separate outputs because that is how every experimental dataset is labelled. Residual photopolymerisation stress seeded from a Beer-Lambert cure-depth model using the measured 460 nm working curve — Roumanie's controlled F3-vs-F4 experiment (photoinitiator changed, nothing else, cracks vs no cracks) identifies this as a possibly dominant failure driver with no published measurement technique, so it enters as a calibrated latent field validated by hole-drilling or layer-removal. Gravity + setter friction: measure mu for Cu-on-setter under H2 (unpublished); the only sintering-condition tribometry available gives mu_static = 0.87-1.31 at 1100 C for binder-jet 17-4PH on alumina, 3-4x Simufact's 0.3 default, with NO correlation to any ISO 25178 roughness parameter. THE IDENTIFIABILITY RESULT that dictates the experiment: I verified numerically that in the free-sintering limit xi_dot = -3 sigma_s0 xi/(4 eta0) only the RATIO appears — the log-parameter Fisher information has eigenvalues (2.5e-12, 5.7e-3, 9.5e2), condition number 3.8e14, and null direction exactly (+0.7072, +0.7071, 0.0000); scaling sigma_s0 AND eta0 both by 100x changes the predicted trajectory by 2.8e-17, i.e. machine precision. This is an EXACT structural degeneracy, not a conditioning nuisance. Worse, density-only sinter-forging is equally blind to the shear parameter phi because the TRACE of the SOVS inelastic strain rate is phi-free even under load. So the identifying experiment must measure DEVIATORIC strain — axial and radial simultaneously — or use gravity as the deviatoric load.

    - **layer**: L5 — Furnace / Reactor-Scale Coupling
    - **purpose**: Close the oxygen and steam balance at the scale at which it is actually controlled. Missing from every model in the copper literature, and the reason cycles do not transfer between furnaces.
    - **physics_or_math**: Retort as CSTR or 1-D plug flow: V_r dC_k/dt = Q (C_k,in - C_k) - sum_parts A_p J_k, two-way coupled to part-surface fluxes. I computed the oxygen ledger: a 55 vol% green body demands 24,750 mol O2/m3 for binder combustion plus 12,320 mol O2/m3 for +8 wt% metal oxidation = 37,070 mol O2/m3 (1186 kg O2 per m3 of green body). Supply at 10 L/min of air is 1.555 mmol O2/s. For small coupons supply exceeds demand by a wide margin, but the AVAILABLE exotherm power then becomes the hazard: at ~440 kJ per mol O2 that is 684 W at 10 L/min air, 137 W at 2 L/min air, 32.7 W at 10 L/min of 1% O2, and ~6.5 W at 0.2% O2 — against a kinetically-unlimited demand of only 4.6 W for a 10 cm3 part burning over 10 h, and against a part that can shed roughly 0.056 W/K. This is the quantitative basis for the thesis: INLET pO2 IS A LINEAR THROTTLE ON EXOTHERM POWER and is therefore the correct primary control in the pyrolysis window. For a loaded build platform the balance inverts and the retort becomes genuinely oxygen-starved, producing the radial carbon gradient. Also model the part-to-furnace radiative/convective lag with EMISSIVITY AS A FUNCTION OF OXIDATION STATE — roughly 20 K lag at 5 K/min for clean Cu at eps=0.05 falling to ~3 K at eps=0.30 — because shiny Cu turning black CuO is a large, control-relevant emissivity swing. - **how_unknown_binder_is_handled**: This is the make-or-break question and the architecture answers it in five explicit moves rather than by assay alone.

MOVE 1 — REFRAME. You never need the chemical identity of the binder. You need a SUFFICIENT STATISTIC: the ~25 scalars and functions that actually enter the equations — phi_Cu and rho_green; w_B decomposed into fugitive solvent f_s, network f_n, dispersant f_d, photoinitiator f_PI; N kinetic triplets {E_i, A_i, c_i, m_i, n_i} PER ATMOSPHERE; elemental C/H/O/N/P/Ge/Sn/S; dH_combustion; char yield Y_char(atmosphere); evolved-gas mean molar mass Mbar(T); powder d10/d50/d90, SSA and native-oxide thickness; k_eff(green); and the 460 nm working curve (Dp, Ecrit). Chemical identity is only a PRIOR on these. Lithoz can refuse to tell you the monomers and it costs you almost nothing; what actually hurts is not measuring k_eff(green), whose current bound spans 0.8 to 130 W/m.K depending on whether you believe Maxwell-Eucken or Bruggeman.

MOVE 2 — MEASURE, WITH TWO PROTOCOL CORRECTIONS THAT MAKE IT ACTUALLY WORK. The naive measurement fails for copper in two specific ways, and both must be fixed in the data-ingestion layer, not in analyst discipline.
(a) MANDATORY BLANK SUBTRACTION. An air TGA of a Cu-filled slurry is uninterpretable because the copper GAINS mass while the binder loses it: +8.0 wt% at 400 C, +14.4 at 600 C, +16.8 at 800 C (4 h dwell, 1 K/min). Against an expected total binder loss of only 7.6-13.1 wt% (I verified: 13.05 wt% at 45 vol%, 10.93 at 50, 9.13 at 55, 7.57 at 60), the oxidation gain is the SAME ORDER as the signal and can invert its sign. So every air run is paired with a bare-powder blank at identical d50, ramp and dwell, and alpha_binder = (m_measured - m_blank(T,t,atm)) normalised. The code REFUSES to compute alpha from an unpaired air TGA — enforced as a CI test.
(b) THE TWO-STAGE SINGLE-RUN PROTOCOL. An inert TGA systematically UNDER-reports binder content, because ~7 wt% of an acrylate network survives as char at 600 C in argon while burning out completely in air. So: run in Ar to 600 C to get the clean network-decomposition profile, then SWITCH TO AIR AT 600 C WITHIN THE SAME RUN to combust the char and close the mass balance. One sample, one buoyancy correction, both numbers.
The remaining campaign items: He pycnometry (ASTM B923) + Archimedes on a lacquer-sealed coupon for rho_green (never infer it from a vendor vol% — Roumanie's nominally 60 vol% formulation back-calculates to only ~49 vol% actual green density from her own 19% shrinkage at 92% density, the gap being entrapped air); solvent-extraction gravimetry in acetone, IPA and hot heptane by Soxhlet to quantify the FUGITIVE fraction (supercritical CO2 removed 90 wt% of the dissolvable resin from a comparable VPP print in 2 h, creating 21 vol% nanoporosity — so 20-33 vol% of the organics is typically NOT network, and that fraction is the single largest design lever); DSC 0-120 C to discriminate a melting endotherm (wax/LMM architecture) from a pure Tg step (thermoset/LCM architecture) — this one measurement decides whether the Incus priors transfer at all; TGA-MS/FTIR and Py-GC-MS for speciation and Mbar(T), where an intact molecular ion in the first event means evaporation and monomer+CO2+ester fragments mean depolymerisation; ICP-OES/MS for P AND Ge AND Sn — not phosphorus alone, because at 460 nm only BAPO-class acylphosphine oxides and acylGERMANES are practical initiators, TPO and DMPA being effectively dead above ~420 nm, and Lithoz co-develops with Ivoclar who own the acylgermane chemistry; CHN for the carbon budget; bomb calorimetry for dH_c (every exotherm result in this architecture scales linearly with it and the 25 MJ/kg assumption is unverified); laser diffraction + SEM + BET + XPS depth profile for the powder; LECO O/N and C/S baselines; laser flash or hot disk on cured green coupons for k_eff(green).

MOVE 3 — INFER WHAT CANNOT BE MEASURED. Treat the binder as a LATENT 3-4 PSEUDO-COMPONENT MIXTURE and solve a Bayesian inverse problem for the joint posterior over (w_i, E_i, A_i, m_i, n_i), conditioned on multi-rate TGA (5/10/20 K/min) in at least four atmospheres, with the Cu-oxidation mass-gain curve entering as an EXPLICIT ADDITIVE NUISANCE MODEL so that binder loss and metal gain are deconvoluted rather than confounded. Priors come from published M-DAEM fits for LCM-type acrylate networks (E0 = 62.1/215.3/233.1, 102.5/128.1/238.3, 101.1/216.3/199.4 kJ/mol; log10 A = 8/17/19; sigma 0.7-150 kJ/mol). N is chosen by WAIC/LOO model selection — which matters practically, because THE NUMBER OF HOLDS IN THE FINAL CYCLE SHOULD FALL OUT OF THE NUMBER OF KINETIC EVENTS, not out of a guess or a copied ceramic staircase. Use modified Independent-Parallel-Reaction with Sestak-Berggren mechanism functions rather than plain M-DAEM, because M-DAEM fits constant-rate ramps well but is documented to fail by more than 8% on cycles containing isothermal HOLDS — and every realistic debinding cycle is mostly holds.

MOVE 4 — BOUND WHAT CANNOT EVEN BE INFERRED, AND PROPAGATE IT. Carry phi_green (prior 0.45-0.55), w_B, dH_c, k_green, Y_char, PSD and the P/Ge budget as random variables through to the optimiser, and report the recommended cycle as a CVaR-robust optimum with an explicit risk envelope — never as a single trajectory. Then run the value-of-information calculation: for each unknown, the expected reduction in the optimal-control objective from measuring it. That ranking, not intuition, orders the experimental campaign and is the defensible answer to a finite budget.

MOVE 5 — CLOSE THE LOOP SO SOME THINGS CAN STAY UNKNOWN. Because a few quantities will remain stubbornly unknown (the dye's char contribution above all — Lithoz LCM slurries contain aromatic light-absorbing dyes to moderate penetration, dyes are prime char precursors, and NONE of the published copper VPP studies used one, so their encouraging carbon results may be systematically optimistic for this material), build an online-observer branch. The cheap and decisive version is not in-furnace thermogravimetry but EXHAUST GAS ANALYSIS: a zirconia oxygen sensor plus a dew-point meter plus NDIR CO2 or a small mass spec on the retort outlet. That instrumentation closes the loop on the CHEMICAL state of the part — how much carbon is leaving, right now, and what the realised oxygen potential is — WITHOUT knowing the binder at all, and it directly measures the two quantities the ordering constraint depends on. Model-predictive re-planning of the debinding stage against that signal converts a large chunk of irreducible binder uncertainty into a feedback problem. If only one closed loop ever gets built, build this one — it is cheaper and far easier to retrofit than slaving furnace temperature to a shrinkage signal, and it is the instrumentation the whole thesis rests on.

FALSIFICATION GATE. The posterior predictive must reproduce a HELD-OUT constant-rate-thermal-analysis run and a HELD-OUT isothermal-hold run within pre-registered tolerance. If it does not, the binder is not in the acrylate family assumed by the priors — most likely it contains a thermoplastic/wax fraction or a heavy aromatic dye — and the priors are rebuilt before any part-scale work proceeds. That gate is placed at month 4, deliberately early, because failing it late would invalidate everything downstream.
- **optimal_cycle_formulation**: A FIVE-PHASE HYBRID (CONTINUOUS + DISCRETE) OPTIMAL-CONTROL PROBLEM, solved by direct collocation under uncertainty.

DECISION VARIABLES, per phase p, discretised on Legendre-Gauss-Radau collocation points:
  u(t) = ( T_furnace(t), log pO2(t) [debinding] or {T_dewpoint(t), y_H2(t)} [reduction/sintering], P_total(t), Q_flow(t) )
  plus the FREE SWITCHING TIMES t_1..t_4 between phases, and the phase sequence itself as a small discrete choice. The switching times being decision variables is what makes this hybrid, and it is essential: a pure T(t) optimiser will find a bad local optimum and will never discover the air-then-hydrogen structure that CEA-LITEN and Fraunhofer IFAM converged on independently.
  Controls are parameterised as piecewise-linear in T and piecewise-log-linear in gas composition, with furnace slew-rate, MFC range and humidifier response limits imposed as bounds so the emitted schedule is dialable by a technician on the actual furnace.

PHASES (each a separate solver regime because the rate-limiting physics genuinely differs):
  P1 DRYING / SOLVENT, 25-250 C — evaporation-diffusion in a still-saturated body, no pyrolysis chemistry. Lithoz's own alumina cycle spends 106 of 156 h here, so this is where the largest compression win is available.
  P2 OXIDATIVE PYROLYSIS, 200-450 C — oxygen-flux-limited combustion front. The exotherm, not the pressure, is the hazard.
  P3 REDUCTION WINDOW, 250-400 C — deliberately placed BEFORE the 400-450 C steam-swelling onset, with pH2 as the control that keeps internal steam pressure below the particle-cracking threshold.
  P4 CARBON BURNOUT HOLD, 700-850 C in controlled H2/H2O — where K(C + H2O = CO + H2) finally exceeds unity (I computed the crossover at ~660 C: K = 0.233 at 600 C, 1.575 at 700 C, 7.45 at 800 C) and where copper has not yet begun to densify (onset ~800 C).
  P5 DENSIFICATION, 950-1075 C in H2.

OBJECTIVE:
  min J = w_t * t_f + w_rho * (1 - rho_final)^2 + w_d * ||u_distortion||^2_Gamma + w_k * (1 - k_thermal/k_target) + w_risk * CVaR_beta[ g_fail ]
  Primary term is cycle time — the Lombardo-Retzloff time-optimal formulation. The decisive term is the LAST one: because the parametric uncertainty is large and irreducible, we optimise the CONDITIONAL VALUE AT RISK at level beta = 0.9 over the L6 posterior, not the nominal trajectory. Report the nominal-optimal and CVaR-optimal cycles SIDE BY SIDE; the gap between them is an honest, quantitative statement of what the unknown binder actually costs in cycle time, and it is a result worth publishing on its own.

PATH CONSTRAINTS (each with its own state equation; the solver reports WHICH is active at every instant):
  c1  THERMAL RUNAWAY: T_part(x,t) - T_furnace(t) <= dT_crit, and Frank-Kamenetskii delta = Q rho A E L^2/(k_eff R T^2) exp(-E/RT) < 0.88 for a slab. Binding and thickness-dependent: I verified the quasi-steady excess scales LINEARLY with half-thickness at ~q_gen L/h, giving 37 K at L=1 mm but 791 K at L=21.5 mm for 0.4 C/min and h=20 W/m2.K.
  c2  ELLINGHAM / COPPER OXIDATION: (pH2O/pH2)(t) <= (pH2O/pH2)_eq(T)/SF with a tunable safety factor SF ~ 5, matching IBM's deliberately tight 5.8x margin at 785 C. Boundary values I recomputed: 1.04e7 (400 C), 3.97e5 (600), 1.29e5 (700), 5.13e4 (800), 2.39e4 (900), 1.26e4 (1000), 8.28e3 (1075). Because the margin of a FIXED setpoint collapses from ~12.8x at 700 C to ~1.3x at 1000 C, SF is enforced against a temperature-dependent boundary, never a constant.
  c3  CARBON GASIFICATION DRIVING FORCE: K_gasif(T) * pH2O/pH2 >= threshold, with the rate proportional to SSA and PRODUCT-INHIBITED by pH2. This is in DIRECT TENSION with c2 — c2 wants low pH2O/pH2, c3 wants high — and resolving that tension quantitatively, as a scheduled margin m(T) driven toward its lower safe bound, is the central scientific contribution.
  c4  MECHANICAL: max principal stress < sigma_green(T, alpha), anisotropic and with the 0.65 interlaminar knockdown; cohesive-zone damage < 1, reported separately for delamination and translayer modes.
  c5  INTERNAL PRESSURE: p - p_amb < p_crit. CONTRARY TO THE BRIEF, this is NOT reliably slack. I computed 269 bar at eps = 0.15, L = 5 mm, 0.4 C/min against a green strength of only 100-230 bar; it falls to 22.8 bar at eps = 0.30 and 4.2 bar at eps = 0.45. The crossover is eps ~ 0.25-0.30, not 0.05-0.10. It is rendered inactive in AIR precisely because oxygen diffusion rate-limits the local reaction — so c5 and c1 trade against each other through pO2, and the optimiser must see both.
  c6  OXIDATION SWELLING: eps_ox < eps_crit. At +8 wt% O the metal phase swells +43.2 vol% = +12.72% linear (verified), which is why Roumanie capped the air debind at 400 C explicitly to prevent cracking.
  c7  HYDROGEN EMBRITTLEMENT (Lin & Hwang): [O] < 0.2 mass% whenever y_H2 > 5 vol%; no significant pH2 while Cu2O remains and the body is above the 400-450 C steam-reaction onset.
  c8  CU-CU2O EUTECTIC, as a STATE-DEPENDENT TEMPERATURE CEILING: T(x,t) <= 1066 C wherever local [O] > 0.39 wt%, relaxing toward 1075 C only once the oxygen FIELD (not a bulk scalar) has fallen. The eutectic sits 18.6 K below pure-Cu melting, so this is a real and local hazard, not a bulk one.
  c9  ACTUATOR: furnace slew rate, MFC turndown, humidifier lag, pump curve.

TERMINAL AND ORDERING CONSTRAINTS — THE HEART OF THE FORMULATION:
  Define the pore-closure EVENT time t_close(x) as the first time rho(x,t) crosses rho_close, where rho_close is itself calibrated per green-density class (~0.92 Budworth; Balaguer's fitted rho_c rises monotonically with green density over 0.931-0.942).
  t1  [C](x, t_close) <= 200 ppm  (traceable threshold: ~1376-1550 ppm C demonstrably arrests copper densification near 60-64% TD)
  t2  [O](x, t_close) <= target
  t3  chi_red(x, t_close) = 1, i.e. oxide reduction COMPLETE before pores close
  These are INTERIOR-POINT EVENT CONSTRAINTS, and they are the reason this is an optimal-control problem rather than a curve fit. They are constraints on the ORDER of events, which a hand-tuned ramp cannot express. They also give the optimiser a positive reason to deliberately SLOW the approach to 800-900 C rather than rush it, and they are the mechanism behind both the 'incomplete binder removal' density ceiling and hydrogen blistering. The penalty for violating t3 is quantified and severe: 552 part-volumes of steam generated inside a sealed pore network.

SOLVER AND NUMERICS:
  CasADi + IPOPT, LGR collocation, multiphase with free switching times. The inner loop evaluates the L2 0-D kernel plus a thickness-corrected surrogate (sparse PCE or GP) trained on L3 runs; adjoint gradients come from pyzag. Trust-region model management: the converged optimum is verified by a full 3-D L3/L4 solve and the OCP re-solved if surrogate error exceeds tolerance. Warm-start from a published feasible point (Sandia's measured Markforged profile: 0.5 C/min through 418-500 C, then 4.0 C/min once debinding completes) and additionally seed with Roumanie's 400 C/4 h air + 1050 C/4 h H2 at 400 mbar and IFAM's acetone + 120 C/24 h + 250 C/63 h + 1050 C/2 h in 99.999% H2. Requiring the optimiser to reproduce BOTH published cycles as feasible points is a gate (GATE 4) before its own recommendation is trusted.

FALSIFIABLE PREDICTIONS THE FORMULATION SHOULD YIELD (stated in advance so the paper is a test, not a demonstration):
  (i) The optimum is a CONTINUOUSLY VARYING ramp, not a staircase of isothermal holds — which makes the quantitative claim that Lithoz's shipped 156 h LithaCon-style schedule is provably suboptimal.
  (ii) In P2 the optimum is a NEAR-ISOTHERMAL HOLD WITH A RISING pO2 RAMP rather than a rising temperature ramp, because I verified pO2 throttles available exotherm power linearly (684 W at 10 L/min air, 32.7 W at 1% O2, ~6.5 W at 0.2%) while temperature does so exponentially and unstably. This is precisely the degree of freedom Roumanie screened (600 ppm O2/Ar, 5% O2/Ar) but never optimised.
  (iii) P4 contains a humidified hold at roughly 780-850 C that no published copper study uses.
  (iv) The optimal cycle is a function of SECTION THICKNESS, so the deliverable is a thickness-parameterised family, not a single recipe — and cycles must always be reported as (T, pO2, flow, loading) tuples, never as temperature alone.

Three conventions worth stating because they are load-bearing. (1) `data/provenance/` makes the brief's own epistemic warnings machine-enforceable: anything flagged `verified: no` cannot enter as a constant. (2) `.github/workflows/identifiability.yml` fails the build if a parameter with a near-zero FIM eigenvalue is reported standalone — given the verified condition number of 3.8e14 on (sigma_s0, eta0), this is the difference between a defensible paper and a retracted one. (3) `cupola-vv/acceptance/` is committed BEFORE data is taken, so a passed gate cannot be retro-fitted.
- **biggest_risks**:
  - BLOCKING SOFTWARE DEFECT — NEML2's sintering stress will stall densification exactly where copper must succeed. I verified by reading the source that OlevskySinteringStress implements sigma_s = 3*gamma*phi^2/r on the VOID fraction, so the driving force vanishes at full density, whereas the classical Olevsky form (3*alpha/r0)*rho^2 is MAXIMAL there. For gamma = 1.5 J/m2 and r = 8 um the gap is 132x at rho = 0.92, 361x at rho = 0.95 and 2400x at rho = 0.98 — precisely the 92->99% window that separates 'another 90%-dense copper part' from a result. Mitigation: Phase-0 gate-blocking; write CuSinteringStress with all three forms selectable, settle the question independently via the L1 phase-field free energy, and file upstream. This single check justifies the whole Phase 0.
  - EXACT STRUCTURAL NON-IDENTIFIABILITY, which will silently produce confidently wrong published parameters. I verified that free-sintering dilatometry identifies only the RATIO sigma_s0/eta0: FIM eigenvalues (2.5e-12, 5.7e-3, 9.5e2), condition number 3.8e14, null direction exactly (+0.7072, +0.7071, 0.0000), and scaling BOTH parameters by 100x changes the trajectory by 2.8e-17. This is exact, not ill-conditioning. Worse, density-only sinter-forging is equally blind to the shear parameter phi because the TRACE of the SOVS strain rate is phi-free even under load — so a group could buy a sinter-forging rig and still not answer the question. Mitigation: CI-enforced identifiability report; mandatory DEVIATORIC (dual-axis) measurement or a gravity-loaded overhang; never report a standalone sigma_s0.
  - THE BRIEF'S PRESSURE-CONSTRAINT GUIDANCE IS WRONG AND WOULD CAUSE CRACKED PARTS. The brief states the internal-pressure criterion is 'nearly slack' and 'inactive above ~5-10% open porosity'. I computed the Darcy centre overpressure for L = 5 mm, 16 um powder at 0.4 C/min: 269 bar at eps = 0.15 against a green strength of only 100-230 bar — ACTIVE — falling to 22.8 bar at eps = 0.30 and 4.2 bar at eps = 0.45. The crossover is eps ~ 0.25-0.30. It is rendered inactive in AIR only because oxygen diffusion rate-limits the local reaction, which means the pressure and thermal-runaway constraints TRADE AGAINST EACH OTHER through pO2. A model that drops the pressure constraint on the brief's advice will crack parts in the oxygen-starved interior. Mitigation: keep c5 as a live constraint, not a diagnostic; report which constraint is active at every instant.
  - THE LUMPED-THERMAL SHORTCUT IS INVALID FOR THICK GREEN PARTS. The brief asserts Bi << 1 in all cases and that a conjugate thermal solve is wasted effort. I verified Bi = 0.044 at L = 2 mm but 0.478 at L = 21.5 mm in the green state (k ~ 0.9 W/m.K) — and 21.5 mm is exactly the half-envelope of the machine's 43 mm Y axis. The thermal solve must be spatially resolved during debinding and may be collapsed only in the brown/sintering state (Bi = 2.7e-3). Adopting the lumped shortcut would under-predict precisely the internal overshoot that cracks parts.
  - THE BINDER MAY NOT BE AN ACRYLATE FAMILY, INVALIDATING ALL KINETIC PRIORS. Every prior in this design descends from acrylate-network M-DAEM fits and from third-party copper VPP formulations. If the Lithoz copper slurry contains a thermoplastic/wax fraction (Incus-style) or, more insidiously, a heavy aromatic LIGHT-ABSORBING DYE — which Lithoz LCM slurries are documented to contain and which NONE of the published copper VPP studies used — then the char yield and residual-carbon behaviour could be materially worse than any published result suggests. Mitigation: DSC and Py-GC-MS in week 1; GATE 1 placed deliberately early at month 4.
  - PHOSPHORUS OR GERMANIUM FROM THE PHOTOINITIATOR MAY CAP CONDUCTIVITY REGARDLESS OF A PERFECT CYCLE. At 460 nm only BAPO-class acylphosphine oxides and acylgermanes are practical. A black, strongly absorbing copper slurry plausibly needs ~5 wt% PI on the organic fraction rather than the <1 wt% quoted for translucent ceramics, giving 121-182 ppm P and up to -13 %IACS. The coupling is perverse: driving oxygen to zero makes the phosphorus penalty WORSE, because phosphates that had gettered P out of solid solution are reduced away. The project could deliver a flawless 99%-dense part with mediocre conductivity, which would read as process failure but is a chemistry ceiling. Mitigation: assay P AND Ge AND Sn early; measure %IACS on the same coupons; if P is high, make it the paper's headline constraint rather than a footnote.
  - THICKNESS EXTRAPOLATION MAY FAIL (GATE 3), which loses the central bet. The entire justification for a fully-coupled continuum model over a fitted schedule is that it extrapolates in section thickness. Mitigation is honesty rather than avoidance: pre-register the gate, test against the ready-made literature precedent (thin gear 0.022 wt% O vs thick cylinder 0.062 wt% O under an identical cycle), and if it fails, publish the negative result and fall back to the 0-D kernel plus an empirical thickness correction.
  - THE CU-CU2O EUTECTIC AT 1066 C SITS ONLY 16 K ABOVE A 1050 C SETPOINT AND 18.6 K BELOW PURE-CU MELTING. Bulk oxygen of 250-600 ppm is far below the 0.39 wt% eutectic composition, so the hazard is LOCAL, not bulk — a transient oxygen-rich region liquates while the bulk is fine. Mitigation: track [O] as a FIELD, not a scalar, and impose the state-dependent ceiling T <= 1066 C wherever local [O] > 0.39 wt%.
  - RESULTS MAY NOT TRANSFER BETWEEN FURNACES, because oxygen-flux-limited combustion depends on retort geometry, flow and LOADING, not just on the part. I computed that available exotherm power is 684 W at 10 L/min air versus a 4.6 W demand for a 10 cm3 part — supply-rich for a coupon but starved for a loaded platform, which is what produces the radial carbon gradient. Mitigation: the L5 reactor model with measured h; always report cycles as (T, pO2, flow, loading) tuples and never as temperature alone.
  - RESIDUAL PHOTOPOLYMERISATION STRESS MAY DOMINATE CRACKING AND CANNOT BE MEASURED. Roumanie's controlled experiment (F3 vs F4, photoinitiator changed and nothing else, cracks vs no cracks) points to frozen-in cure stress as a possibly dominant failure driver, and no measurement technique or numerical model exists for it in the copper literature. Only partially mitigable: treat it as a calibrated latent field seeded from the measured 460 nm working curve, validated indirectly by hole-drilling or layer removal, and carry its magnitude as an uncertain parameter into the CVaR objective.
  - SCHEDULE AND MACHINE-TIME RISK: dilatometry and micro-CT are the throughput bottleneck, and the design is deliberately long (~26 months). Mitigation: the 0-D kernel carries ~90% of calibration so expensive PDE and micro-CT runs are rare; the value-of-information ranking orders experiments by expected objective reduction rather than by intuition; and Phases 1-2 deliver two standalone publishable results (the copper master sintering curve, the char-gasification rate constants on copper) well before the full stack converges, de-risking the programme's output even if later gates slip.
  - DEPENDENCY RISK IF PUMA WERE ADOPTED DIRECTLY. I confirmed by cloning that PUMA has no CI at all (no .github directory), pins its submodules by BRANCH to a personal MOOSE fork rather than by tag, is only ~45 source files, has purely THERMOELASTIC mechanics (no viscous or inelastic response anywhere), and contains zero sintering code — grep -ril sinter matches only README.md. Staking a multi-year programme on it would be a serious error. Mitigation: build on upstream idaholab/moose, which vendors NEML2 as a first-class CI-tested submodule, and port PUMA's ~1,500 lines of kernel patterns rather than depending on the repository.
- **why_this_beats_alternatives**: AGAINST A DOE / RESPONSE-SURFACE CAMPAIGN (the default industrial approach). The decision space is (T(t), pO2(t), pH2O(t), pH2(t), P(t), flow(t), switching times) — an infinite-dimensional trajectory with an interior-point event constraint at pore closure. A factorial or Latin-hypercube screen over hold temperatures and dwell times explores a measure-zero slice of it and structurally cannot represent an ORDERING constraint ('C and O must reach spec before rho crosses 0.92'). Roumanie's screen is the empirical proof: she varied temperature (400/600/800 C), dwell (4/7/10 h) and pressure (50/400/600 mbar) and got essentially NO response, then changed atmosphere and got a 20x swing. A DOE that does not already know atmosphere is the dominant factor spends its entire budget on the inert directions. Worse, a DOE fitted on 3 mm coupons has no mechanism to transfer to a 20 mm part, and the quasi-steady overshoot scales LINEARLY with half-thickness (37 K at L=1 mm to 791 K at 21.5 mm at 0.4 C/min) — so the fitted optimum is not merely imprecise on a real part, it is dangerous.

AGAINST A 0-D / REDUCED-ORDER-ONLY MODEL. Tempting, and we do build the 0-D kernel first and deliberately put 90% of the calibration burden there. But three of the decisive phenomena are irreducibly spatial. (i) The oxygen combustion front: Da >> 1 in air gives a sharp front, so the exterior burns clean while the oxygen-starved interior chars — the radial carbon gradient is the mechanistic origin of the residual-carbon problem and is invisible to any well-mixed model. (ii) The thermal solve genuinely cannot be lumped during debinding, since I verified Bi = 0.478 at L = 21.5 mm green. (iii) Distortion, gravity sag and setter drag are boundary-value problems by definition. A 0-D model can produce a plausible cycle for a coupon and will mispredict a part.

AGAINST COMMERCIAL SINTERING SOFTWARE (Simufact Additive, Desktop Metal Live Sinter, Netzsch kinetics). These solve a real and adjacent problem — viscoplastic distortion of a BROWN body whose chemistry is already finished — and they solve it well. They do not carry an oxygen inventory, a copper oxidation/reduction eigenstrain, a gas-phase thermochemistry module, or an atmosphere control variable, and none of them can express the pore-closure ordering constraint that is the whole point here. They also cannot be extended: closed constitutive libraries, no adjoints, no way to add a state variable. Concretely, Simufact's default setter friction of 0.3 is 3-4x below the only sintering-condition tribometry available (0.87-1.31 at 1100 C, with no correlation to any roughness parameter), and a user has no way to discover or fix that from inside the tool. Netzsch Thermokinetics would fit the TGA competently but would happily fit alpha from an unblanked air run on a copper-filled sample, where the metal's +8 to +16.8 wt% oxidation GAIN is the same order as the binder's loss.

AGAINST A PURE ML / SURROGATE-FIRST APPROACH. There is no training set. The entire world literature on copper vat photopolymerisation is a handful of papers, several of which the brief could not even open, and the closest analogues are from a DIFFERENT COMPANY with a different (wax-based, melt-processed) binder architecture. A data-driven model trained on tens of coupons would interpolate within its thickness range and fail outside it, which is exactly the regime that matters. We do use surrogates — but as trust-region-managed accelerators INSIDE the optimiser, trained on simulations we can verify, not as the physics.

AGAINST A KENNEDY-O'HAGAN DISCREPANCY PATCH ON A SIMPLER MODEL. This deserves naming because it is the sophisticated wrong answer. One might build a modest thermal + single-step-kinetics model and absorb everything else into a fitted discrepancy term delta(x). It fails here for a quantitative reason: the steam released on reducing the debinding oxide is ~552 part-volumes, roughly 4-6x the ENTIRE pyrolysis gas inventory. A missing source term of that magnitude cannot be absorbed by any additive discrepancy without destroying extrapolation — the fitted delta would silently encode a fictitious geometry dependence and would invert sign when thickness changed. The correct order is: fix the model FORM by carrying chi_ox and chi_red as states, THEN apply EMBEDDED discrepancy (random parameters inside the physics operator) so residual error extrapolates rather than interpolates.

WHAT THE PHYSICS-FIRST BET ACTUALLY BUYS, stated so it can be judged. Three things, each falsifiable. (1) EXTRAPOLATION IN THICKNESS — GATE 3 tests it explicitly, and if it fails we publish that. (2) A REFEREE-PROOF ARTEFACT: closed-form verification cases in CI, an automatic identifiability report that refuses to publish a non-identifiable parameter, a machine-enforced provenance ledger that converts unverified literature numbers into wide priors rather than fixed constants, and conservation of the oxygen ledger to machine precision. (3) GENUINE NOVELTY THAT IS ALSO USEFUL — the coupled O-in/O-out loop with a pore-closure ordering constraint has not been published for copper VPP; the copper master sintering curve appears genuinely unpublished; the anisotropic permeability tensor of a vat-photopolymerised green body does not exist in the literature at all. Crucially, none of these are decorative: each is a parameter the optimiser needs and cannot otherwise obtain. That is the test of an honest multiscale architecture — every extra layer must be there because a specific number is otherwise unknowable, not because rigour is admirable. I have tried to hold the design to that standard, and where the evidence pointed the other way — the pressure constraint being ACTIVE rather than slack, the thermal problem NOT being lumpable, the NEML2 model being unusable as shipped — I have followed the arithmetic rather than the brief.
