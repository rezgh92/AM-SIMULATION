    - **answer**: Much later than the recon assumed, which materially relaxes the carbon-removal deadline.     - **question**: Is the Cu-Cu2O eutectic at ~1065 C actually reached in practice?

- **actionable_for_architecture**:
  - Make the atmosphere a TEMPERATURE-DEPENDENT CONTROL TRAJECTORY, not a constant. The design variable is the reducing margin m(T) = (H2/H2O)_actual / (H2/H2O)_eq(T), and the optimiser should drive m toward its lower safe bound to maximise carbon gasification. The margin collapses from 12.8x at 700 C to 1.3x at 1000 C for a fixed H2/H2O = 1e-4, so a constant setpoint is unsafe. Implement (H2O/H2)_eq(T) = K_H2O(T) * sqrt(pO2_eq(T)) as a first-class constraint function with an explicit, tunable safety factor, and expose the Arden Buck dew-point conversion as the actuator model.
  - Replace the pO2-window framing with a THREE-FIELD coupled transport-reaction model inside the part: carbon (solid char), oxygen (as Cu2O plus dissolved O, with the 28-57 wt-ppm solubility ceiling), and the gas phase (H2, H2O, CO, CO2, CH4). Because copper catalyses the water-gas shift, the gas-phase sub-model must include CO + H2O <-> CO2 + H2 as a fast surface reaction; that caps internal pCO far below the ~1e5 atm equilibrium value and creates an in-situ H2 source that feeds blistering. Do not assume frozen gas-phase chemistry.
  - The dominant source term in debinding is VOLUMETRIC GAS GENERATION, not chemistry: 240-840 cm3 of gas per cm3 of green body at 400 C (best estimate ~400-500x part volume for 50-55 vol% Cu). Build the debinding model as Darcy/Knudsen flow through an evolving pore network with a pressure-based cracking criterion, driven by an independent-parallel-reaction pyrolysis source. Permeability, not reaction rate, is the limiter; heating rate is the control. This is why Lithoz specifies 0.05-0.25 C/min between 115 and 250 C.
  - Use the modified Independent Parallel Reaction (IPR) form with a Sestak-Berggren mechanism function, not a plain DAEM. The published comparison shows M-DAEM fits constant-rate TGA well but fails on cycles containing isothermal HOLDS (>8% error), and every realistic debinding cycle contains holds. Seed with the published 3- or 5-component parameter sets (E0 62-238 kJ/mol, log10 A 8-19) and refit against TGA-MS of the actual Lithoz slurry at 5/10/20 K/min in four atmospheres.
  - Track SPECIFIC SURFACE AREA as a state variable alongside relative density. Carbon gasification and oxide reduction are both area-proportional, and SSA collapses long before open porosity closes at ~92%. Pore closure is NOT the deadline the recon assumed (it occurs only near 1050 C for a 52-58% green body); SSA collapse is. Couple SSA to the sintering model so the optimiser sees the real closing window.
  - Model oxide reduction with an INCUBATION term. Cu2O reduction by H2 is nucleation-controlled with an apparent Ea of ~114.6 kJ/mol and a long induction period (hours at low temperature); surface-science work gives ~72 kJ/mol for the propagation step once metallic fronts exist. Use a JMAK/Avrami form with a nucleation delay, not a first-order Arrhenius rate, or the model will predict reduction far too early and hide the blistering risk.
  - Model blistering as CREEP-DRIVEN VOID GROWTH, not fracture. The 579-1138 MPa figures are thermodynamic ceilings (p = RT/dV, dV = 9.66 cm3/mol H2O) that relax as soon as the void dilates. Implement: local H2O generation rate from oxide reduction -> pressurised void -> power-law creep dilatation of the surrounding copper -> healing during the subsequent densification hold (the source paper explicitly observes crack healing). Couple this to the same viscoplastic constitutive model used for sintering shrinkage so blistering and densification compete in one solver.
  - Use a Skorohod-Olevsky Viscous Sintering (SOVS) continuum viscoplastic model with an Arrhenius viscosity function for the sintering/distortion stage, including gravity and part-setter friction. This is the approach validated for binder-jetted metals (Simufact, Desktop Metal Live Sinter) and it is the only formulation that will reproduce the gravity sag and setter-drag distortion a 76 x 43 x 170 mm LCM part will show. Anchor densification against the Ott regression (+1% green density -> +0.21% sintered density over 56-72% loading) and against 95.3% at 52% loading / 1050 C / 2 h / H2.
  - Carry the GREEN-STATE GRADIENT into the thermal model. Above ~20 vol% solids the 460 nm light penetration collapses, so each layer has a steep through-thickness cure gradient and the green body has anisotropic, layer-correlated density and degree-of-cure variations. Cure depth also depends on powder oxidation state (reduced powder cures deeper). The simulation should accept a non-uniform green density field from the printing model rather than a single scalar.
  - Split the cycle into distinct atmosphere/pressure regimes and let the optimiser choose the switch temperatures: (1) RT-300 C, gas-evolution-limited, low pressure / high flow, mildly reducing; (2) 300-700 C, char formation and early oxide reduction; (3) 700-950 C, the humidified gasification window at maximum pH2O and minimum pH2 with the margin constraint active; (4) >=950 C, backfill to >=10 kPa and switch to H2 for final reduction and densification. Switch temperatures, dwell times, pH2O(T), pH2(T) and total pressure are the decision variables.
  - Add a hard constraint at the Cu-Cu2O eutectic: 1066 C, 0.39 wt% O. Sintering at 1050 C leaves only 16 C. Ott's 1075 C is above it and only works if local oxygen is genuinely low. The optimiser must not be free to raise the sintering temperature above ~1055 C unless the oxygen field is proven below ~50 ppm everywhere.
  - Include copper evaporation as a boundary condition, not an afterthought: Langmuir recession 0.2/3.2/10.4/20.1 um/h at 900/1000/1050/1080 C, suppressed to ~1/0.1/0.01 um/h at 100 Pa/1 kPa/10 kPa. For a 30 um-pixel part this is a dimensional-accuracy term, and total pressure during the hold is therefore an optimisation variable with a lower bound of ~1 kPa.
  - Add a PHOSPHORUS-OXYGEN COUPLING sub-model for the property prediction, since it is a genuine trade-off the optimiser can exploit. Phosphorus (30-270 ppm from BAPO) costs 0.73 %IACS per 10 ppm ONLY while it is in solid solution; in oxygen-bearing copper it is gettered into Cu3(PO4)2/Cu2P2O7, which are more stable than Cu2O and cannot be reduced by any atmosphere that protects copper. So the conductivity objective is non-monotonic in residual oxygen. Model k_eff = f(porosity via Maxwell-Eucken, P in solution, residual C), not k = f(porosity) alone.
  - Set the debinding SPECIFICATION at <200 ppm carbon entering the sintering hold (stretch <100 ppm), justified by US5302562: ~1550 ppm C gives zero densification near the melting point and 1376 ppm gives 63.7% from a 62% green. IBM targeted <300 ppm; Roumanie achieved 180 ppm. Make residual carbon a hard constraint in the optimiser, not a soft objective.
  - Build in the Cu/Cu2O SOLID-STATE OXYGEN BUFFER as a hardware option and as a boundary condition in the model. IBM's copper-oxide setter tiles act as a self-regulating buffer that pins the local atmosphere on the Cu/Cu2O line and cut binder burnoff from ~30 h to 18 h. A Cu/Cu2O powder bed in the retort is a cheap, high-value experimental arm and removes most of the atmosphere-control risk from the tightest part of the cycle.
  - Adopt ASTM B577 (850 C, 10% H2, 20-40 min, then polarised-light metallography and a cold bend) as the standing acceptance test for hydrogen-embrittlement susceptibility, and use interrupted-quench Archimedes-vs-helium-pycnometry divergence as the in-process measure of cracking/closed porosity (the method that resolved the swelling mechanism in the Lin & Hwang study). Both give the simulation cheap, repeatable validation targets.
  - For calibration and validation, prioritise these published anchors and encode them as regression tests: (1) 52% loading, 1050 C, 2 h, H2 -> 95.3% TD; (2) 58% loading, same cycle -> 92.7%, closed spherical porosity; (3) 12 um powder, 850 C/6 h + 1075 C/6 h H2 -> >99%; (4) 60 vol% DLP copper, air debind 400 C + H2 at 980/1030/1050 C, 3 C/min, 400 mbar, 4 h -> ~90% TD, 180 ppm C, 250 W/m.K; (5) MIM copper ceiling ~97% TD and 380 W/m.K.
