- **corrections**:
  - MAJOR — the recon's central process assumption is inverted. It frames debinding as a reducing-atmosphere problem with a dew-point window to be found. The only peer-reviewed study of debinding an acrylate-bound, highly-loaded copper photopolymer found exactly the opposite: at 400 C/4 h, hydrogen leaves 0.25 wt% C, argon 0.394 wt%, and vacuum, 600 ppm O2/Ar and 5% O2/Ar all leave ~0.36 wt%, while AIR leaves 0.019 wt% C — the same as the raw powder. Acrylate networks char, copper forms no carbide, and the char cannot be gasified at debinding temperatures in a reducing gas. The proven route is OXIDISING debind at ~400 C then reduce the copper in H2 during the sinter ramp. Both independent LCM/DLP copper groups (CEA-LITEN and Fraunhofer IFAM) use air debinding. (Roumanie et al., SN Appl. Sci. 3:55 (2021), Fig. 6 and Table 3.)
  - MAJOR — the claim that 'for a 30 um-pixel LCM slurry the powder is almost certainly finer (likely D50 3-10 um)' is unsupported and probably backwards. Every published vat-photopolymerisation Cu feedstock uses D50 = 16-22 um, and Scheibler explicitly measured that cure depth INCREASES with particle size. The recon's downstream conclusion — that 1080 C cycles are 'far too aggressive' and 900-1000 C should be the starting point — rests on that wrong premise. The Herring speed-up is 0.3-4.6x between real Cu feedstocks, not 27x.
  - MAJOR — 'Sandia used SOVS for Markforged copper' is an overstatement. SAND2023-10043R never names SOVS; its model reference [5] is Paudel, Deng & To, 'A physics-based data-driven distortion compensation model for sintered binder jet parts considering size effects', Additive Manufacturing 68 (2023) 103517, and the report describes only 'classical sintering kinetics' with grain growth, shrinkage and viscoplastic distortion. Sandia DOES have a verified SOVS implementation, but in a different report (SAND2017-12933R, DOI 10.2172/1411315) and calibrated to 0.2 um ZnO, not copper.
  - MAJOR — the recon mis-attributes the Sandia bending failure to high-temperature slumping. The report states the specimens 'were failing at low temperatures', in the brown window between binder removal and the onset of sintering — they FRACTURED, they did not sag. The dominant risk for LCM copper is brittle failure and cracking of the brown body (CEA-LITEN saw 'horizontal and vertical cracks' in three of four formulations and traced them to polymerisation stress in the acrylate network, not to the thermal cycle), not incipient melting near 1085 C. Three groups sinter LCM/DLP Cu at 1030-1080 C with no slumping reported.
  - The recon's SOVS constitutive equation is garbled. Its prefactor '2*eta/(1-theta)' does not appear in the Sandia/Olevsky form, which is sigma'_ij = 2*eta0*phi(rho)*e_dot'_ij. Its phi and psi forms are correct (phi = (1-theta)^2 = rho^2; psi = (2/3)(1-theta)^3/theta = (2/3)rho^3/(1-rho)), and so is sigma_s0 = 3*alpha/r0, but the deviatoric prefactor must be fixed before it goes in a UMAT. Using the XY factor cubed instead of the full anisotropic product adds a further 1.4-point error on the Incus data (54.8 vs 53.4 vol%).
  - 'What dew point simultaneously reduces Cu2O and oxidises residual carbon' is posed as the key unanswered thermodynamic question; it is answerable in closed form from JANAF data and the answer is that the Cu2O constraint does not bind at all (K = p_H2O/p_H2 = 1.1e7 at 400 C down to 1.0e4 at 1050 C). Only the carbon side has a real threshold, at about 650 C. Treating it as an open question overstates the difficulty and, worse, hides the actionable conclusion: dew point is a free design variable, and raising it is the lever for carbon removal above 700 C.
  - 'Oxidised Cu emissivity ~0.6-0.8' is too high for the state the part will actually be in. - **actionable_for_architecture**:
  - Restructure the whole system around the OXIDATION-REDUCTION CYCLE, not around a diffusion-only sintering model. The dominant physics is: (1) air debind burns the binder AND oxidises 60%+ of the copper, expanding the metal phase by ~43 vol% (+12.7% linear) with a combined binder+oxidation exotherm of 110-120 W/kg; (2) the H2 ramp reduces it back, releasing 5 mol (112 L STP) of steam per kg of copper that must escape before pore closure at ~92%; (3) only then does densification proceed. A simulator that models diffusion-driven densification but not the oxide cycle will mispredict shrinkage, cracking and residual oxygen. Make oxidation extent an explicit state variable coupled to oxygen ingress, and validate it against the three TGA anchor points (+8, +14.4, +16.8 wt% at 400, 600, 800 C).
  - Build the thermal model as 0-D lumped, not as a conjugate heat-transfer PDE. Biot = 1.2e-4 (brown) to 9e-3 (green): the part is isothermal. Spend the compute budget instead on (a) the furnace-to-part radiative/convective lag with emissivity as a function of oxidation state — 20 K lag at 5 K/min for clean Cu at eps = 0.05, falling to 3 K at eps = 0.30 — and (b) internal gas transport, which is the actual rate limiter. The PDE set you need is reaction-diffusion for O2 in / binder vapour out / H2 in / H2O out through an evolving porous medium, coupled 1-way to a lumped energy balance with source terms from the two exotherms.
  - Adopt the Sandia-verified SOVS form verbatim for the mechanics branch, with a fully implicit backward-Euler integrator at beta = 1.0 (beta = 0.5 gives non-physical oscillatory stresses) and a mesh fine enough to resolve curvature — the report is explicit that warpage cannot be captured on a coarse mesh. Treat sigma_s0 = 3*alpha/r0, the viscosity coefficients a4/b4/c4, and the grain-growth law as CALIBRATION parameters fitted to your own coupons, exactly as Sandia calibrated theirs against vertical displacement of bend bars. Do not chase gamma_s from first principles; it is a lumped fitting constant in this framework.
  - Split the optimisation into two decoupled sub-problems with different objectives and different physics, because they are governed by different rate limits. Sub-problem A (debinding, RT to ~500 C, air or low-pO2): objective = complete binder removal and controlled oxidation with no thermal runaway and no crack initiation; constraint = local self-heating and the 12.7% linear oxidation swelling; decision variables = ramp rates, hold temperatures, oxygen partial pressure, section thickness. Sub-problem B (reduction + sintering, 500-1075 C, H2 with controlled dew point): objective = maximise density and minimise residual O and C; hard constraint = finish reduction before pores close at ~92%. Only sub-problem B needs SOVS; sub-problem A is a reaction-diffusion-thermal problem. A monolithic 'one optimiser over one cycle' architecture will waste most of its effort.
  - Make DEW POINT a first-class control variable in the optimiser, with a two-stage schedule. Below ~650 C run dry (steam does nothing for carbon and only slows oxide reduction marginally); above ~700 C deliberately humidify. Concrete candidate to simulate and then test: air debind 400 C, then WET H2 (dew point about 0 to +10 C, i.e. ~1 vol% H2O) held at 800-850 C where the water-gas constant is 19-50 and pores are still wide open, then dry H2 or 4%H2/Ar for a final densification hold at 1050-1075 C. This is the single most likely locus of a novel publishable contribution, it is thermodynamically grounded, and no published Cu study has tried it.
  - Anchor the first experimental campaign on the IFAM recipe rather than on binder-jet literature, because it is the closest published analogue: 52-58 vol% loading, 16 um powder, 25 um layers, 200 mJ/cm2, acetone solvent debind 24 h, air debind 24 h/120 C + 63 h/250 C, sinter 2 h/1050 C in 99.999% H2 on porous alumina. Then use CEA-LITEN's 400 C/4 h air + 1050 C/4 h H2 at 400 mbar as the second arm. Both are fully specified, both reach 90-95% density, and the difference between them (air-only 92% versus solvent+air 95.3%) isolates debinding completeness as the controlling variable — which is exactly the variable the unknown Lithoz binder hides.
  - Instrument for the failure mode that actually occurs: brittle fracture and cracking in the brown/early-sinter window, not slumping. That means the Gibson-Ashby branch (E*/Es = C1*(rho*/rhos)^2, sigma*/sigma_ys = C2*(rho*/rhos)^1.5) and a fracture criterion matter MORE than the viscoplastic sag branch for this process. Design a supported pre-sinter step into the workflow from the start (Sandia needed one; note it cost them 6 density points, 91% vs 97%, so treat it as a last resort for slender geometries rather than a default).
  - Add a CONTAMINATION model alongside the thermal one. Carbon and oxygen are solved problems for this chemistry (0.018 wt% C and 0.022-0.067 wt% O are demonstrably achievable), but PHOSPHORUS from a BAPO photoinitiator plus DHP powder will cap conductivity at ~250 W/m/K no matter how good the cycle is, and it is invisible to every thermal measurement. Before any conductivity claim: LECO C/O/N plus ICP-OES for P, Ti, Co, Fe, As, Si on sintered coupons. Ask Lithoz the one narrow question that does not require them to disclose the formulation — is the photoinitiator phosphorus-free?
  - Make wall thickness an explicit model input and an explicit design rule, because oxygen removal is thickness-limited. CEA-LITEN's thin-walled gear reached 0.022 wt% O (raw-powder level) while their bulk disc from the identical cycle reached 0.062 wt%. That is a 3x difference from geometry alone. The optimiser should therefore return a cycle CONDITIONED ON the maximum section thickness of the part, not a single universal recipe — and the deliverable to the group should be a thickness-to-cycle map.
  - Plan the master-sintering-curve measurement as a deliverable in its own right, but fit it as a TWO-BRANCH curve. No MSC for pure copper appears to exist in the literature, so 4-5 constant-rate dilatometry runs (2, 5, 10, 20 K/min to 1075 C in 4%H2/Ar on fully debound LCM coupons) is both the fastest route to an optimum cycle and a publishable result. Fit two Q values across the GB/lattice crossover at ~780 C rather than forcing one sigmoid, and run a bare-powder blank alongside so the oxidation mass-change signal can be subtracted.
  - Define the calibration coupon set up front, because one sintered coupon pins several unknowns at once: print bars in three orientations at a known nominal loading; measure green and sintered dimensions by CMM in all three axes (gives Sx, Sy, Sz and hence the anisotropy that is completely unpublished for Cu); Archimedes density; then invert phi = rho_rel/(Sx*Sy*Sz) for green density. Cross-check that inverted green density against a direct TGA-to-constant-mass in air on green feedstock, CORRECTED for the +8 to +25 wt% copper oxidation mass gain — an uncorrected ash test will report a solids loading badly too high.
  - Keep the Frost-Ashby constants as the single source of truth for D(T) and mu(T), with the honest uncertainty attached: delta*D0b and Qb for Cu are Frost & Ashby's own ESTIMATES (delta*D0b = b*D0v, Qb = 0.6*Qv per footnote h), carrying perhaps a factor of 3 in delta*Db. Build that as an explicit uncertainty band in the model rather than a point value, and cross-check against Surholt & Herzig, Acta Mater. 45 (1997) 3817 before publication. Every derived quantity in the recon that rests on these was re-checked here and is arithmetically correct.
