  - WRONG EXPONENT ASSIGNMENT IN A PUBLISHED TABLE (and the recon cites it in a way that propagates the error). ICTAC 2022 Table 3 lists the random-scission truncated Sestak-Berggren parameters under column headers 'c, m, n' with values 1.204, 1.119, 0.4 for L=2. Combined with ICTAC's own Eq. (1.4), f(alpha) = c alpha^m (1-alpha)^n, that reads f = 1.204 alpha^1.119 (1-alpha)^0.4 - which is WRONG. The exact L=2 solution f = 2(alpha^0.5 - alpha) behaves as alpha^0.5 as alpha -> 0 and as (1-alpha)^1 as alpha -> 1, so the exponent on alpha must be ~0.4 and the one on (1-alpha) ~1.1. The ORIGINAL source (Sanchez-Jimenez et al., Polym. Degrad. Stab. 95 (2010) 733, Table 2) labels the same three columns 'c, n, m' - i.e. ICTAC transposed the m and n headers. My own least-squares fit of the exact numerical f(alpha) returns c=1.131, m(on alpha)=0.371, n(on 1-alpha)=1.068 for L=2, settling it. The recon's NUMERICAL assignment is correct, but its source field points at ICTAC Table 3, which as printed says the opposite; anyone who follows that citation will code the wrong model. This matters concretely: the two forms differ most at small alpha, which is exactly the induction region that sets the debinding onset and the pre-percolation pressure spike.
  - THE PBT TRIPLET IN THE RECON HAS ITS EXPONENTS SWAPPED. The recon reports 'f(alpha) = alpha^1.138 (1-alpha)^0.294'. The paper says n = 1.138 and m = 0.294 in the convention f = c alpha^m (1-alpha)^n, and states the result explicitly as '(1-alpha)^1.138 alpha^0.294'. So the correct form is f(alpha) = alpha^0.294 (1-alpha)^1.138. The recon's version is also internally inconsistent with its own (correct) random-scission assignment, since the paper's whole point is that PBT's f(alpha) closely resembles a random-scission model - which alpha^0.294(1-alpha)^1.138 does and alpha^1.138(1-alpha)^0.294 does not.
  - THE RECON CONFLATES TWO DIFFERENT SANCHEZ-JIMENEZ PAPERS. It attributes the PBT triplet, the PTFE/PE triplets, and the experimental conditions to one paper titled 'Combined kinetic analysis of thermal degradation of polymeric materials under any thermal pathway'. The experimental conditions the recon lists for PBT (9 mg, 70 cm3/min N2, ramps 1/2/5 K/min, CRTA at 8.3e-4 min^-1, isotherm at 583 K) are correct and do belong to the PBT paper - but the attribution is muddled.
  - THE CENTRAL OPTIMISATION CLAIM IS FALSE. The recon states that the CRTA/SCTA trajectory 'by construction minimises the maximum decomposition rate for a given cycle duration, hence the peak pyrolysis-gas generation rate and peak internal pressure - the quantity that cracks parts'. The second 'hence' does not follow. Peak pressure is proportional to (dalpha/dt)/kappa(alpha), and kappa varies by three to four orders of magnitude over the cycle as the pore network percolates (Vg^3 dependence). A constant-rate programme therefore produces a pressure history that is enormously front-loaded, and wastes almost the entire cycle running far below the allowable stress. The correct control law is constant STRESS (constant dp, or constant dp/sigma_f(T)), under which dalpha/dt must RISE by three to four orders of magnitude. Quantified: for d50 = 1 um, 10 mm thick, 1 MPa limit, the constant-pressure cycle takes 5.5 h versus 188 h for the constant-rate cycle with the SAME peak pressure. This is a 34x error in the recommended cycle length. CRTA is still the right EXPERIMENTAL tool (one-run f(alpha) fingerprinting, step separation, third slice of the kinetic surface) - just not the right production trajectory.
  - THE COPPER-CATALYSIS EVIDENCE IS OVERSTATED AND MIS-CHARACTERISED. (a) The recon gives it 'high' confidence; the underlying system (PMC8469408) is a carboxylic CATION-EXCHANGE resin with Cu(II) ion-exchanged onto carboxylate groups plus CuO deposits at up to 35 wt% Cu. That is a copper carboxylate salt, not micron metallic Cu powder dispersed in a crosslinked acrylate network, and copper carboxylates decompose by their own route. Confidence should be MEDIUM for transfer to your system. (b) The specific claim that copper 'in nitrogen convert[s] a two-stage decomposition into a four-stage one' is not what the paper says - it reports that all the copper-loaded resins decomposed in four stages in N2 'as in air'. (c) The '~100% more carbon char' figure is verbatim and correct, and the '100-150 C' downshift in air is verbatim and correct.
  - THE PRACTICAL INFERENCE ABOUT ATMOSPHERE IS BACKWARDS. The recon frames inert debinding as the char trap and implies that air must be avoided because copper oxidises. The empirical literature says the opposite is the winning route: debinding in AIR at 400 C followed by hydrogen sintering gives 0.018-0.019 wt% C, while debinding in HYDROGEN gives 0.25 wt% C - a 13x penalty (Roumanie et al.). The copper oxidation incurred in air (+8 wt% at 400 C) is fully reversible under H2 while porosity is open; the char is not removable at all below Cu's melting point unless an oxidant is supplied. The thermodynamic reason is that Cu2O + C -> 2Cu + CO has dG < 0 at every temperature, so the deliberately grown oxide is a built-in internal oxidant for the residual char. The recon had all the pieces (no carbide, char trap) but drew the wrong operational conclusion.
  - THE M-DAEM PARAMETERS SHOULD NOT BE USED AS PRIORS, AND THE PRESSURE PREDICTION THE RECON QUOTES IS PARTLY AN ARTEFACT. Four defects in Wang et al., Materials 18 (2025) 4004, all verified in the full text: (1) the pre-exponential factors were FIXED at 1e8 / 1e17 / 1e19 s^-1 and not fitted, so the reported E0 values are conditional on that choice and the 'global R2 > 0.9999' carries no validation weight; (2) the TGA used a SINGLE heating rate of 5 C/min, which is precisely the ICTAC prohibition the recon cites elsewhere ('a single non-isothermal TG curve covers no area of the 3D kinetic surface'); (3) S2's second pseudo-component has sigma = 150.14 kJ/mol against a mean E0 of 128.12 kJ/mol, i.e. a Gaussian with a large negative-activation-energy tail - physically meaningless and a clear symptom of non-identifiability (S3's third component, sigma = 72.94 on a mean of 199.43, is nearly as bad); (4) S1's first pseudo-component (E0 = 62.11, A = 1e8 s^-1) has a computed DTG peak at 44.6 C at 5 K/min and 18.2 C at 0.5 K/min - below the TGA start (35 C) and far below the 373.15 K start of their transport simulation, so 3% of S1's binder is released instantaneously at t=0 in the transport model. The S2 and S3 peaks (0.34 MPa at 352 C, 0.22 MPa at 355 C) are physically located and my independent scaling reproduces them, so those two are sound.
  - THE SIGNAL-TO-NOISE WORRY IS MISPLACED AND THE REAL TGA PROBLEM IS DIFFERENT. The recon says that because the binder is only ~11 wt% of a copper green body, 'signal-to-noise, buoyancy and blank correction dominate'. The ICTAC rule is about mass LOSS, not sample mass: a 9.2 mg copper sample gives exactly the recommended ~1 mg loss and satisfies mass x beta <= 100 mg C/min up to beta = 10.9 K/min, so ordinary sampling works. Buoyancy is handled by routine blank subtraction. The real, and much more serious, problem is specific to copper: in any oxygen- or water-bearing atmosphere the copper GAINS mass (+8 to +16.8 wt% measured), so the TG trace is the sum of a burnout sink and an oxidation source and does NOT give alpha at all. Conversion must be recovered from evolved-gas analysis (TG-MS or TG-FTIR, integrating the carbon-bearing species) or by differencing against a matched inert-filler control. Any kinetic analysis that takes alpha = (m_t - m_0)/(m_f - m_0) straight off a copper TG curve in a non-inert atmosphere is invalid.
  - THE THERMAL-GRADIENT ARGUMENT IS RIGHT BUT INCOMPLETE, AND THE OMISSION IS DANGEROUS. The recon notes that copper's high conductivity suppresses internal dT relative to a ceramic. True for conduction. But it omits the exotherm: the binder's heat of complete combustion is ~25 MJ/kg (Huggett's constant with HDDA stoichiometry), and because copper's specific heat is only 0.385 kJ/kg/K, the adiabatic temperature rise for a 50 vol% Cu green body burning all its binder in air is ~5070 K. A metal-filled compact is therefore MORE prone to thermal runaway per unit binder than a ceramic one, not less. In practice air debinding of a thick copper part is oxygen-supply limited (209 g O2, about 159 L of air at STP, per kg of green body), and the correct model treats the oxygen flux into the part as the rate-limiting step and the oxygen concentration as a control variable - not as a fixed atmosphere.
  - ONE NUMBER I COULD NOT VERIFY. The recon's claim that 'FWO with the Doyle approximation can deviate by up to ~35% from differential methods when Ea varies strongly' is attributed to PMC6631284 at medium confidence. I could not verify the 35% figure from any primary source I could reach; ICTAC itself states the existence of the systematic error without quantifying it. Mark this unverified. The Doyle constants (5.331, 1.052), the Miura-Maki constant (0.6075) and the Starink form (T^1.92, 1.0008) are all standard and correct as stated.
  - A CONTRADICTION IN THE LITERATURE THE RECON DID NOT FLAG. Wang et al. (Materials 2025) state that shrinkage of VPP green bodies during debinding is 'less than 2%'. Conti et al. measured by TMA on Lithoz LithaCon 3Y 610 an 11% linear shrinkage after debinding (their programme included a pre-sinter) and 29% after sintering. Do not assume the brown body is dimensionally stable through debinding; measure it by TMA/dilatometry on your own material, because whatever shrinkage occurs while the binder is softening is the regime in which slump and distortion occur.
- **actionable_for_architecture**:
  - MAKE THE STATE VECTOR ELEMENTAL, NOT JUST alpha. Because copper forms no carbide and gains mass by oxidation, a single conversion variable is structurally inadequate. Carry, per control volume: n_C (carbon in solid char), n_O (oxygen as Cu2O/CuO plus adsorbed), n_H, n_P and n_S (non-volatile poisons that no cycle removes), plus the binder pseudo-component conversions alpha_i, plus gas-phase partial pressures (pO2, pH2, pH2O, pCO, pCO2, hydrocarbons), plus porosity Vg and relative density. Residual C, O and P are the actual objectives of the optimisation; alpha is only an intermediate. This is the single biggest structural difference from a ceramic debinding code.
  - REPLACE CRTA WITH CONSTANT-STRESS OPTIMAL CONTROL AS THE TRAJECTORY GENERATOR. The control law should impose dp(alpha,T,t) = eta * sigma_f(T) with eta a safety factor, giving dalpha/dt_allowed(alpha) = eta sigma_f 2 kappa(alpha) P M / (mu R T V_b rho L^2), and then invert the kinetics to get the temperature programme, T(alpha) = E_alpha / (R ln[A_alpha f(alpha) / (dalpha/dt)_allowed(alpha)]) for a single step, or solve sum_i c_i A_i exp(-E_i/RT) f_i(alpha_i) = (dalpha/dt)_allowed numerically for the multi-step case. This is the recon's CRTA machinery with the right right-hand side, and it is worth 30x in cycle time at identical risk. It is also the publishable contribution: 'constant-stress (rather than constant-rate) sample-controlled debinding for highly permeable metal green bodies'.
  - RESOLVE THE PERCOLATION TRANSITION EXPLICITLY. Use Vg(alpha) = Vg0 + V_binder * alpha in the Kozeny-Carman expression and make the solver adaptive in alpha, with most of its resolution in 0 < alpha < 0.05, because permeability rises ~1000x across that interval and that is where the entire pressure risk lives. Treat Vg0 (the as-printed closed porosity, ~0.03-0.05) as a calibrated parameter, not a constant - it depends on cure shrinkage, trapped air and cleaning. Implement a percolation threshold below which the Kozeny-Carman form is replaced by a bubble-nucleation / liquid-pressurisation sub-model; below percolation Darcy flow is simply the wrong physics.
  - TREAT POWDER d50 AS THE MASTER SENSITIVITY AND GET IT FIRST. Permeability scales as d^2, sintering stress as 1/d, and specific surface area (hence surface oxide per unit mass, hence oxygen inventory) as 1/d. A factor of 5 in d50 moves peak pressure by 25x and decides whether the part densifies at all (German's threshold stress of 0.57 MPa for copper sits between d = 10 um and d = 22 um). Measure it yourself by laser diffraction and SEM on the decanted, solvent-washed slurry; do not wait for Lithoz. Structure the code so d50 and the PSD width are top-level inputs with automatic sensitivity sweeps.
  - BUILD ATMOSPHERE IN AS A DECISION VARIABLE WITH A THERMODYNAMIC FEASIBILITY LAYER. The optimisation variables are not just T(t) but the schedule of (pO2, pH2, dew point, total pressure). Wrap the solver in a thermodynamic layer that evaluates, at every (T, gas composition): Cu/Cu2O stability, C + H2O = CO + H2, the Boudouard reaction, Cu2O + C, Cu2O + H2, and methanation - and that refuses infeasible set-points. The useful, non-obvious feasibility results already computed: inert gas at any achievable dew point is OXIDISING to copper below ~1000 C; any hydrogen-bearing gas keeps copper metallic up to ~0.5 vol% O2 equivalent; carbon gasification by steam opens at ~690 C. Total pressure belongs in the variable set too, since peak dp scales as 1/P - debinding under a few bar of gas is a legitimate and cheap lever.
  - USE A COMPETITIVE MULTI-STEP SCHEME, NEVER KINETIC DECONVOLUTION. Because the oxidative and pyrolytic branches compete and their branching ratio depends on heating rate and on local pO2, MDA/KDA is formally invalid (ICTAC Sec. 4.4). Implement the competitive form dalpha/dt = sum_k A_k exp(-E_k/RT) f_k(alpha) h_k(pO2, pH2O), with the isoconversional diagnostic Ea_alpha = (E1 r1 + E2 r2)/(r1 + r2) used as a consistency check. Use random-scission f(alpha) (corrected exponents) or truncated Sestak-Berggren for the network scission step, not n-order - and remember that F1 fits ramp data at r > 0.99 while being order-of-magnitude wrong on isothermal holds, which are exactly what a dwell-based cycle is made of.
  - ADD A GAS-PHASE SECONDARY CRACKING TERM. Binder vapour cracks to soot above ~600 C, inside the pore network, on the copper you are trying to keep clean. Model it as a sink on the hydrocarbon partial pressure and a source on n_C, driven by local T and pore residence time tau = L^2 mu / (kappa dp) or the equivalent from the resolved velocity field. This imposes a hard constraint the kinetics alone will never produce: alpha must exceed ~0.98 before any point in the part passes 550-600 C in a non-oxidising atmosphere.
  - MAKE OXYGEN SUPPLY, NOT KINETICS, THE LIMITER IN THE AIR-DEBINDING BRANCH. The binder needs 209 g O2 per kg of green body and releases 25 MJ/kg of binder; the adiabatic rise is ~5000 K. Couple the energy equation to an oxygen-diffusion/convection sub-model at the part surface and inside the pore network, with the burnout rate min(kinetic rate, O2-supply rate). Expect - and design for - a sharp reaction front moving inwards, which is the classic metal-compact burnout morphology, and add a runaway-detection constraint (dT_local/dt above some threshold) to the optimiser.
  - IMPLEMENT A POROSITY GATE FOR HYDROGEN, NOT A TEMPERATURE GATE. Steam from internal reduction of an enclosed Cu2O inclusion reaches 580-1100 MPa against a 10-100 MPa yield strength. Encode the rule as: hydrogen permitted while the open-porosity fraction exceeds a threshold (roughly relative density below 0.92-0.95), oxygen inventory n_O must be driven below a set point before pore closure, and any admission of H2 after pore closure with n_O above that set point is flagged as a hard constraint violation. This is a constraint on the joint (densification, atmosphere) trajectory and is why the sintering and debinding models must be solved together, not sequentially.
  - DESIGN THE EXPERIMENTAL CAMPAIGN AROUND THE FOUR ICTAC SLICES PLUS A COPPER-SPECIFIC CONTROL SET. Programmes: five ramps spanning a factor of ten (e.g. 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10 K/min) with sample mass halved for the two fastest, at least two isothermal holds, and at least two CRTA runs at different C. Sample: ground green body below 1 mm, ~9-13 mg (giving the ICTAC-recommended ~1 mg loss), purge 100 cm3/min vertical or 200-300 horizontal, explicit E-vs-mass test. Copper-specific additions that are NOT optional: (i) an inert-filler control at matched d50 and volume fraction, (ii) a pre-reduced air-free copper arm to separate metallic Cu from surface-oxide catalysis, (iii) TG-MS or TG-FTIR on every run because TG mass is not alpha for copper, (iv) a pO2 ladder at <1, 10, 100, 1000 ppm and air to test whether the contributions c_i drift with heating rate. Fit all curves simultaneously by combined kinetic analysis to one triplet per branch; never fix A.
  - BUILD THE PARAMETER LAYER AS BAYESIAN FROM THE START. Binder content, solid loading, powder size, Vg0, the Kozeny constant and the green strength are all unknown to within factors. Do not chase a single deterministic optimum. Represent each as a prior, propagate to the cycle recommendation, and report the cycle as a robust one (e.g. satisfying the stress constraint at the 95th percentile of the posterior). Check identifiability explicitly - the kinetic compensation effect makes E and ln A nearly collinear, so profile-likelihood or an MCMC sample is required before any parameter is quoted with an uncertainty. Use AIC/BIC plus the heating-rate invariance of c_i to fix the number of pseudo-components; refuse to go beyond what the data support.
  - VALIDATE ON A LADDER, AND MAKE THE LADDER PART OF THE CODE. Level 0: reproduce the Si3N4 VPP benchmark (my scaling gives 0.357 MPa against their 0.20-0.34 MPa - keep that as a regression test). Level 1: TGA/CRTA reconstruction of runs held out of the fit. Level 2: TMA/dilatometry on the real material through debinding and sintering, giving the shrinkage history the model must reproduce. Level 3: two-thickness discs (2 mm and 10 mm) at one heating rate to confirm the L^2 pressure scaling. Level 4: interrupted runs with LECO C/O, ICP-OES for P and S, and Raman for char structure, closing the elemental mass balance that TG cannot close. Level 5: the real 5-20 mm geometry. Benchmark the final cycle against the published Route 3 (air 400 C 4 h, then H2 to 1050 C) - if the optimiser cannot beat 0.018 wt% C and 0.067 wt% O, it has not earned its keep.
  - KEEP MATERIAL PROPERTIES AS ELEMENT-WISE FIELDS EVEN FOR SINGLE-MATERIAL RUNS. It costs nothing now and is the only way the 2M30's multi-material capability becomes usable later. The dominant multi-material risk is differential sintering strain rate at the interface, so the sintering model (Skorohod-Olevsky viscous sintering or equivalent) must accept spatially varying bulk and shear viscosities and sintering stress from day one.
  - PLAN THE PAPER AROUND THE THREE GENUINELY NEW CLAIMS, NOT AROUND 'WE DID A SIMULATION'. (1) Constant-stress beats constant-rate sample-controlled debinding by more than an order of magnitude for highly permeable metal green bodies, with the crossover set by the d^2 dependence of permeability - this is general and no one has stated it. (2) For copper the binder-removal optimum is oxidative-then-reducing, and the mechanism is that the deliberately grown Cu2O is a stoichiometric internal oxidant for the char via a reaction that is favourable at every temperature - which turns an apparent process defect into the carbon-removal mechanism. (3) A kinetic-plus-transport model for a system where the filler is chemically active, closed by an elemental rather than gravimetric mass balance. Each is defensible with the data you can generate in one campaign.
- **still_unknown**:
  - The Lithoz copper slurry's volumetric solid loading. This is the one number to ask Lithoz for explicitly; they can give it without disclosing any chemistry, and everything downstream scales with it. If they refuse, invert it from a single TGA plus He pycnometry on a cured green body (the LithaNit 720 cross-check shows this is reliable to ~1 wt%).
  - The copper powder d50, PSD width, morphology and oxygen content. This is the master sensitivity (permeability d^2, sintering stress 1/d, oxide inventory 1/d) and it is measurable by you in an afternoon - laser diffraction and SEM on the decanted, solvent-washed slurry, plus LECO O on the recovered powder.
  - Whether the photoinitiator is a phosphine oxide (BAPO/TPO family). If it is, residual phosphorus at the few-hundred-ppm level will destroy conductivity and no cycle will remove it. Ask Lithoz the yes/no question; verify by ICP-OES or GDMS for P and S on a sintered coupon.
  - Whether the binder contains a non-reactive plasticiser, oligomer or thermoplastic phase. Resolvable without disclosure by Py-GC/MS plus TGA-FTIR/MS on the cured green body, plus a solvent-extraction test on the cured film (a non-reactive component will extract).
  - Whether metallic copper powder - as distinct from copper carboxylate or CuO - catalyses THIS binder's decomposition, and in which direction. No published data exists for the actual system; only the matched inert-filler control will answer it.
  - Quantitative kinetics (not thermodynamics) of C + H2O -> CO + H2 on carbon supported on copper at 700-900 C: rate constants, order in pH2O, and whether copper catalyses or poisons the gasification. I found the thermodynamic window and qualitative PM-furnace evidence but no rate data. This is a real gap and would itself be publishable.
  - Quantitative kinetics of Cu2O reduction by H2 inside a porous compact as a function of temperature, pH2 and pore size - i.e. how long the reduction hold must be before porosity closes. Thermodynamics says 'always favourable' and tells you nothing about the time you need.
  - Measured gas permeability of an LCM/VPP green or partially debound body. Every published number I could reach is a Kozeny-Carman estimate, not a measurement. A simple flow-through permeameter on partially debound discs at several alpha values would pin the Kozeny constant and is the highest-value single measurement for the transport model.
  - Green-body tensile/flexural strength and creep behaviour of the copper compact as a function of temperature through the binder-softening window. This sets the actual failure criterion and the slump limit, and no analogue exists in the ceramic literature because ceramics are three times lighter.
  - The minimum controllable heating rate and the mass-loss or evolved-gas feedback available on the group's actual debinding furnace - which decides whether a true sample-controlled (constant-stress) programme can be run in closed loop, or whether it must be pre-computed and played back open-loop as a piecewise-linear approximation.
  - Whether the 2M30 campaign will actually co-print a second material. If yes, the differential-shrinkage co-sintering problem becomes the dominant failure mode and needs its own model and its own experiments; I found no published data on multi-material copper LCM.
  - Delamination strength at LCM layer interfaces for a metal-filled slurry - whether the interlaminar plane is a preferential failure path under internal pressure or sintering stress. Measurable with notched green specimens but not addressed anywhere I could find.
