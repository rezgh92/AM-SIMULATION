Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Co-firing copper in a cordierite-type glass-ceramic printed by multi-material vat photopolymerisation: a model-based process design", for publication as a full-length article in the *Journal of the European Ceramic Society*.

Multi-material vat photopolymerisation can now print a ceramic body and its conductors as one green part. For packages that need low-loss dielectrics and copper wiring in three dimensions, this would remove the planar constraints of tape lamination. Whether such a part can be fired is an open question. Carbon has to leave the glass before its pores close, copper has to stay metallic, and the two materials have to shrink compatibly. These three requirements compete for the same temperature range. To our knowledge, no study has addressed them together for a printed part.

The manuscript addresses them with a model that couples binder chemistry, viscous sintering and crystallisation of the glass, diffusional sintering of copper, and the mechanics of co-firing, in one dimension and on the printed voxel grid in three dimensions. The model is verified against closed-form solutions. Its inputs are taken from the literature, each with a stated provenance. The glass is calibrated against the published IBM glass-ceramic/copper process, and the calibrated model reproduces the atmosphere and the burnout temperature of that process. The paper then:

- maps the atmosphere and temperature window in which carbon can be removed before the glass seals;
- shows that the difficulty of this material pair lies in the order in which the two materials shrink, rather than in their final shrinkage;
- designs the copper paste and the furnace programme together;
- ranks the uncertain inputs by their influence, which defines the measurements that should come first.

The work is computational. We state this plainly in the manuscript, and we discuss its limitations in a dedicated section. We believe it will interest readers working on co-firing, LTCC and additive manufacturing of ceramics for electronics.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission, and there are no competing interests to declare.
