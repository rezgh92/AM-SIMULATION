\begin{abstract}
Multi-material vat photopolymerisation can print a glass-ceramic body and its copper conductors as one green part, but whether such a part can be fired is unknown. Carbon must leave the glass before its pores close, copper must stay metallic, and the two materials must shrink compatibly, all within the same temperature range. We address these requirements with a verified model coupling pyrolysis and steam gasification of char, viscous sintering and crystallisation of the glass, diffusional sintering of copper, and co-firing mechanics, analytically and on the printed voxel grid in three dimensions. The inputs come from the literature, and the glass viscosity was calibrated against the published IBM glass-ceramic/copper process, whose atmosphere and burnout temperature the model reproduces. Only steam, with hydrogen held at a fixed fraction of the Cu/Cu$_2$O equilibrium, removes the char before the glass seals. Fine copper then densifies during the burnout, well before the glass, and the free-strain mismatch reaches 15.6\%. A paste and programme optimised together exploit the retarding effect of char on copper to release both materials at once and cut the mismatch to 3.7\%, but this optimum is sensitive to the gasification rate and the glass viscosity. %% ABS_ROBUST Plastic flow of the copper limits the cooling bow of a 100~$\mu$m copper plane on 1~mm of glass-ceramic to 13~$\mu$m over 20~mm. Char gasification kinetics, glass viscosity, copper sintering viscosity and char yield should be measured first.
\end{abstract}
