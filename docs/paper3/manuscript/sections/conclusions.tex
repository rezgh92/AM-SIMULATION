\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

A model of binder removal, sintering and crystallisation was used to design the co-firing of copper inside a cordierite-type glass-ceramic printed as one part by multi-material vat photopolymerisation. It was verified against closed-form solutions, and its glass was calibrated against the published IBM glass-ceramic/copper process, with whose atmosphere and burnout temperature it is consistent. No experiments were made. The main conclusions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Of the atmospheres examined, only steam removes the carbon from the glass before its pores close. Hydrogen tied to the steam at a fixed fraction of the Cu/Cu$_2$O equilibrium keeps the copper metallic and is consistent with the IBM operating point. At 780~$^\circ$C such an atmosphere gasifies char seven times faster than humidified forming gas, which leaves more than 300~ppm of carbon in the glass-ceramic in every programme examined.
\item Fine copper and the glass densify in different temperature ranges: 450--700~$^\circ$C for 3~\textmu m copper against 850--930~$^\circ$C for the glass. Co-fired with the IBM-type programme, the copper densifies during the burnout, the free-strain mismatch reaches 15.6\%, and the damage index of a buried line exceeds 1. The final shrinkages differ by only 4\%; the difficulty lies in the order in which the two materials shrink.
\item If char holds copper back, as the model assumes, the burnout step also releases the copper to sinter. The best paste and programme found exploit this coupling: a short, hot burnout just below the coalescence of the glass releases both materials together and reduces the peak mismatch to 3.7\%. The design is sensitive to this timing. Halving the gasification rate, shifting the glass transition by 15~K or changing the strength of the carbon effect by a factor of four to five restores one half to two thirds of the mismatch that the optimisation removed, and the 24\% filler limits the copper to 64\% IACS.
\item A design required to meet every constraint under a factor of two either way in the gasification rate combined with $\pm15$~K in the glass transition uses 7~\textmu m copper with almost no filler and a 2.8~h burnout at 806~$^\circ$C. At all four corners of that uncertainty it keeps the peak mismatch between 9.1 and 11.4\%, the copper at 89\% IACS and the carbon at glass closure below 50~ppm, in a 16~h cycle, and it is insensitive to the assumed effect of carbon on copper. The robustness costs a factor of two and a half in nominal mismatch.
\item In three dimensions, a buried copper plane that densifies before the glass acts as a rigid inclusion. The baseline test vehicle shrinks by 15\% in plane instead of 23\%, and its upper face dishes by 0.6~mm. The nominal optimum reduces the dishing to 0.15~mm and the robust design to 0.32~mm, the same ranking as the one-dimensional mismatch.
\item On cooling, plastic flow of the copper limits the stress it transmits to its yield stress. A 100~\textmu m copper plane on 1~mm of glass-ceramic bows by 13~\textmu m over 20~mm, about a nineteenth of the elastic prediction.
\item The inputs that decide the outcome are, in order, the gasification kinetics of the char, the viscosity of the glass, the sintering viscosity of the copper and the char yield. All four can be measured on single-material printed coupons, and they should be measured before the process is tried.
\end{enumerate}
