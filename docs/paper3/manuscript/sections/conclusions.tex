\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

A model of binder removal, sintering and crystallisation was used to design the co-firing of copper inside a cordierite-type glass-ceramic printed as one part by multi-material vat photopolymerisation. It was verified against closed-form solutions, and its glass was calibrated against the published IBM glass-ceramic/copper process, whose atmosphere and burnout temperature it reproduces. No experiments were made. The main conclusions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Of the atmospheres examined, only steam removes the carbon from the glass before its pores close. Hydrogen tied to the steam at a fixed fraction of the Cu/Cu$_2$O equilibrium keeps the copper metallic and reproduces the IBM operating point. At 780~$^\circ$C such an atmosphere gasifies char seven times faster than humidified forming gas, which leaves more than 300~ppm of carbon in the glass-ceramic in every programme examined.
\item Fine copper and the glass densify in different temperature ranges: 450--700~$^\circ$C for 3~$\mu$m copper against 850--930~$^\circ$C for the glass. Co-fired with the IBM-type programme, the copper densifies during the burnout, the free-strain mismatch reaches 15.6\%, and a buried line is overloaded in tension. The final shrinkages differ by only 4\%; the difficulty lies in the order in which the two materials shrink.
\item Char holds copper back, so the burnout step also releases the copper to sinter. An optimised paste and programme exploit this coupling: a short, hot burnout just below the coalescence of the glass releases both materials together and reduces the peak mismatch to 3.7\%. The coincidence is sharp. A factor of two in the gasification rate, or 15~K in the glass transition, restores most of the mismatch, and the 24\% filler it requires limits the copper to 64\% IACS.
\item %% ROBUST_CONCL
\item On cooling, plastic flow of the copper limits the stress it transmits to its yield stress. A 100~$\mu$m copper plane on 1~mm of glass-ceramic bows by 13~$\mu$m over 20~mm, about a nineteenth of the elastic prediction.
\item The inputs that decide the outcome are, in order, the gasification kinetics of the char, the viscosity of the glass, the sintering viscosity of the copper and the char yield. All four can be measured on single-material printed coupons, and they should be measured before the process is tried.
\end{enumerate}
